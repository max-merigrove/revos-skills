# VSL Writer

You are writing the script for a video that sells. The viewer landed on a page with a
headline, this video, and a button or form below it. Your script has one job: get the
right person to press that button, and let the wrong person leave early.

This skill is built for people who are not copywriters. Follow it in order. Do not skip
the intake, because a VSL written from guesses sounds like every other VSL and converts
like one too.

Source of the method: Jeremy Haynes's VSL flow (Megalodon Marketing), with Merigrove's
copy rules layered on top.

---

## Step 1: Collect the facts (do not write yet)

A VSL is a sales call with no one on the other end. Everything a good salesperson would
say, ask, and answer has to be in the script. You cannot invent any of it.

Read `references/intake-brief.md` and work through it with the person. Ask in plain
language, a few questions at a time, not the whole list at once. If the conversation,
attached files or project knowledge already answer a question, take the answer from there
and confirm it in one line instead of asking again.

**Hard stops.** Do not draft until you have real answers for these six. If the person
says "just make something up" for any of them, explain in one sentence why you can't
(price and proof decide whether the video works) and ask again.

1. Who is speaking and what they sell (name, business, the offer's name).
2. Who the buyer is: their identity, the situation they are in, and roughly how
   financially established they are. This decides the length of the video.
3. The result the buyer wants and what they have already tried that did not work.
4. The method: what is different about how this offer gets the result, and why that
   works where the usual approach fails.
5. The offer itself: price (or price range if the person insists), what the buyer gets
   (the real deliverables, not benefits), and the format.
6. What happens after the video: apply, book a call, buy. And what the button says.

**Soft inputs.** Ask for them, but a marked placeholder is acceptable if the person has
nothing yet: credibility numbers, testimonials, guarantee or refund policy, the questions
prospects ask on sales calls, who the offer is not for, and how traffic reaches the page
(cold ads, organic, email).

**Write the one-line belief before drafting.** From inputs 3 and 4, write one sentence:
"The best way to get [result] is [this way], because [why]. Not [what they do now],
because [why it fails]." Read it back to the person. If they would not say it out loud,
fix it before writing. The whole video argues for this sentence. If the
`one-belief-selling` skill is available, use it here and take the belief from its belief
bank when the client already has one.

## Step 2: Pick the length

The person's answer to "how financially established is the buyer" sets the shape:

| Buyer | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Established, decides fast, buying to make or save money (founders, business owners, high-ticket B2B) | 2 to 10 minutes | Sections 1 to 8 only. Fast, direct, no second pass. |
| General public, first-timers, buying with savings, needs to think about it | 15 to 45 minutes | Sections 1 to 8, then the skeptic pass (sections 9 to 14). |

Two adjustments:

- **Cold traffic** (paid ads to strangers) wants a shorter video with more credibility and
  more explaining of the opportunity. **Warm traffic** (email list, followers) can skip
  most of the credibility and go harder on the offer. If the page gets both, write the
  cold version; it is the one that has to earn attention.
- Sections 1 and 2 together should take under a minute, whatever the total length. The
  data in the source training shows most drop-off happens in the first four minutes, and
  it comes from people talking too long about themselves before saying what the video is
  about.

Tell the person which version you are writing and why, in one line, then write.

---

## Step 3: Write the script

Read `references/vsl-flow.md` for what each section does, how long it runs, the trap to
avoid, and example lines. The sections in order:

**Action-taker half (every VSL)**

1. **Hook: what you can help them with.** Who it's for, the result, in two or three
   sentences. No warm-up, no "in this video I'm going to".
2. **Credibility: why you.** Specific, believable numbers and milestones. Only what is
   relevant to the claim you just made. Backstory only if it is the proof.
3. **Sell the opportunity.** Why this result matters now, and why this way of getting
   it beats the alternatives they are considering. This is where the one-line belief
   lives, explained as cause and effect, not stated.
4. **The offer, price first.** "Here's how I help you do this. It costs X. Here's what
   comes with it." Then the real deliverables, one by one. No value stack, no made-up
   "normally $10,000" numbers.
5. **Objections.** "The most common questions people ask when they hear that are..."
   Then answer them one at a time, including the price question.
6. **Who it's for, who it's not for.** Said plainly. This is where the wrong people
   leave, which is the point.
7. **Call to action.** One action, said once, with what happens next.
8. **Proof.** Three or four testimonials, real ones. In the script, write where they go
   and what each one shows; mark them as placeholders if the person has none yet.

**Skeptic half (long version only)**

9. **Guarantee or refund policy**, if one exists. Never invent one.
10. **More proof.**
11. **More objections**, using the follow-up method: each answer creates the next
    question, so answer that one too.
12. **Expectations**: what the buyer has to do, what happens in the first weeks, what
    a realistic timeline looks like.
13. **Proof again.**
14. **Final call to action.**

### How to write each line

- **Write for the ear.** Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Contractions are fine.
  Read every line out loud in your head; if it stumbles, cut it.
- **Third-grade reading level.** Plain words. If a term needs explaining, explain it in
  the next sentence.
- **Speak to one person.** "You", never "you guys" or "everyone watching".
- **Explain, do not claim.** "This works" is a claim. "This works because X, so Y, which
  is why Z" is an explanation. Use the second.
- **The method is what and why. The offer is how.** Tell them why the approach works.
  Do not teach the steps, templates or tools; that is what they are paying for.
- **Numbers are real or marked.** Every number in the script came from the person or is
  written as `[PLACEHOLDER: real number for X]`. Example numbers used to paint a picture
  are introduced as examples ("say you close one call in five").
- **No em dashes anywhere.** Use a full stop, a comma, or "..." for a pause.
- **Banned words:** "proven", "guaranteed" (unless describing an actual guarantee),
  "game-changer", "unlock", "skyrocket", "transform your life", "secret", "hack".
- **No AI tells:** "Here's the thing", "Let me walk you through", "In today's video",
  "Without further ado", "dive in", "actually" as filler, stacked "it's not X, it's Y"
  lines.
- **No client names in copy.** Results and first names only, unless the person confirms
  they have permission.

## Step 4: Format the output

Deliver the script in this shape, every time:

```
# [Offer name] VSL script
Version: [short / long] · Target length: [X] min · Traffic: [cold / warm / both]
One belief: [the one sentence]

## 1. Hook (0:00 to 0:20)
[spoken lines]

## 2. Credibility (0:20 to 0:50)
[spoken lines]

... (one block per section, with time stamps)

---
## On-screen notes
- [what to show at each moment: price card, deliverables list, testimonial screenshots]

## Placeholders to fill before recording
- [each PLACEHOLDER with what is needed and where it goes]

## Page notes
- Headline above the video: [one line that continues the ad's promise]
- Button text: [the CTA]
- Below the video: keep it empty on first launch; add the deliverables list and proof
  screenshots only if play rate is low or cost per booked call is high
```

Spoken lines are the only thing in the section blocks. No stage directions inside the
spoken text, no "[pause for effect]", no labels like "Hook:" inside the copy itself.

If the person asks for the script as a document, deliver the same content in the format
they asked for.

## Step 5: QA before handing over

Run `references/qa-checklist.md` against the draft. Fix what fails. Then tell the person
in two or three lines what you changed and what is still a placeholder. Do not deliver a
script that fails the first five checks on that list.

If the `gary-the-copy-chief` prompt or a copy QA skill is available in the project, run
the finished script through it after your own QA.

## When the person wants something other than a fresh script

- **"Review / fix my VSL"**: map their existing script to the 14 sections, show which
  are missing or out of order, then rewrite in the same length band. The most common
  fixes are: hook takes too long, price is hidden or comes last, a value stack, no
  "who it's not for", testimonials that look polished rather than real.
- **"Make it shorter"**: cut sections 9 to 14 first, then trim 2 and 3. Never cut 4, 6
  or 7.
- **"Make it longer"**: add the skeptic half. Do not pad the action-taker half.
- **"Write the page too"**: headline continues the ad's promise and names the result;
  sub-headline says who it's for; video; button. Nothing else until data says otherwise.
- **"Write the ads to this"**: hand off to an ads skill if one is available, and tell it
  the ad must frame the viewer to watch a video ("I made a short video that explains...")
  because that is what lifts play rate.

# VSL intake brief

Collect these before writing. Ask in groups of three or four, in plain words. Pull
answers from files, project knowledge or earlier messages when they are already there,
and confirm them in one line rather than asking again.

Why this matters: a VSL is every sales call the person will never get to have. If the
script does not know the price, the deliverables, the buyer's situation and the method,
it will fill the gaps with generic lines, and generic lines are what makes a viewer
leave at second fifteen.

---

## A. The speaker and the offer (hard stop)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Your name, your business name, the name of the offer | The hook opens with it. "My name is X, I help Y do Z." |
| In one sentence, what does the offer get the buyer? | This is the result in the hook and the headline above the video. |
| Is this a course, a program, done-for-you, coaching, software, a product? | Decides how section 4 lists deliverables and how section 12 sets expectations. |

## B. The buyer (hard stop)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Who is this for? Describe one real person: what they do, where they are in their business or life, what stage they are at | The hook calls them out. "Who it's for" (section 6) repeats it. |
| How established are they financially? Are they buying with business money to make or save money, or with personal savings? | Sets the length. Established buyers want 2 to 10 minutes. General public wants 15 to 45 minutes and a skeptic pass. |
| What are they doing right now to get this result that is not working? | Section 3 has to explain why that fails, fairly, without blaming them. |
| What else are they considering instead of you? | Section 3 compares your way to the alternatives they are weighing. |

## C. The method (hard stop)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What is different about how you get the result? | This is the "opportunity" the video sells before the offer. Without it the offer is a commodity. |
| Why does that work where the usual approach fails? Explain it like you would to a friend | Section 3 is an explanation, not a claim. You need the cause and effect from the person. |
| What do people usually get wrong about this problem? | The misconception you correct in section 3. |

Write the one-line belief from these answers and read it back:
"The best way to get [result] is [this way], because [why]. Not [what they do now],
because [why it fails]."

## D. The offer (hard stop)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What does it cost? Any payment plan? | Said out loud, first, in section 4. No exceptions. Hiding price fills the calendar with people who cannot buy and demoralises the sales team. |
| List everything the buyer gets. Real items: calls, modules, done-for-you work, access, community, support, timeline | Section 4 lists what comes with it. Benefits are not deliverables. "Clarity" is not a deliverable; "a 60 minute call where we map your next 90 days" is. |
| How long does it run? What is the format? | Expectations in section 12, and a common objection in section 5. |

## E. What happens next (hard stop)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| After watching, what should they do: apply, book a call, buy? | The CTA. One action only. |
| What is below the video on the page: a form, a calendar, a checkout button? | The script tells them what they will see and do. |
| What happens after they take that action? Who contacts them, when? | Removes the "then what?" friction that stops people clicking. |

## F. Credibility (soft: placeholder allowed)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Years doing this, number of clients or students, money made or saved for clients, your own results, where you have been featured | Section 2. Specific, believable, relevant. Only what connects to the claim in the hook. |
| Is there a short backstory that explains why you can do this? | Use only if it is the proof. "Where I'm from" is not proof. "I ran the same kind of business for eight years" is. |

If none of these exist yet, write `[PLACEHOLDER: credibility number]` and tell the
person what to fill in. Never invent a number.

## G. Proof (soft: placeholder allowed)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Testimonials: videos, screenshots of messages, written quotes? With names and faces? | Section 8 and the skeptic pass. The script marks where each one goes and what it shows. Screenshots from where they happened (a chat, an email) are more believable than polished graphics. |
| Specific results with real numbers? | One or two in section 3 to back the method, if they exist. |

## H. Objections (soft, but strongly recommended)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What questions do people ask on sales calls or in DMs before they buy? | Section 5. These are the real objections. Guessing produces generic ones. |
| Why do people say no? | Same. |
| What do people worry about after they buy? | Section 12 (expectations) in the long version. |

If the person has no call data yet, use these defaults and label them as guesses:
why that price, is there a payment plan, how long until results, how much time does it
take me, why this format and not a cheaper one, what if it doesn't work for me.

## I. Who it is not for (soft)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Who do you not want on a call? Who gets bad results with this? | Section 6. Saying it plainly is what keeps the calendar clean. |

## J. Guarantee (soft)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is there a refund policy, a guarantee, or a condition under which you keep working for free? | Section 9. Only if it exists and only as it is actually written. |

## K. Traffic (soft)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| How do people reach this page: paid ads to strangers, your email list, your followers, referrals? | Cold traffic needs a shorter video with more credibility and opportunity. Warm traffic can skip most of section 2. |
| Will the ad tell them there is a video to watch? | It should. "I made a short video that explains..." lifts play rate. Note it for the ads. |

## Filled example (fictional offer, for shape only)

```
A. Speaker: Dana Reyes, Reyes Bookkeeping Academy. Offer: "Advisory Upgrade".
   Result: a bookkeeper with 10+ clients adds a monthly advisory service at $500 to
   $1,500 per client. Format: 8 week group program plus 1:1 reviews.
B. Buyer: a bookkeeper 3 to 8 years in, 10 to 30 clients, billing $40 to $80 an hour,
   working too many hours. Buying with business money. Trying: raising hourly rates
   (clients push back), adding more clients (more hours). Considering: a CFO
   certification course ($8K, 6 months, no sales training).
C. Method: package three reports the bookkeeper already produces into a monthly
   advisory call, and sell it to existing clients first. Works because the data is
   already there and the trust is already there, so there is no new skill to learn
   and no cold selling. The certification route fails because it adds knowledge, not
   a sellable product, and the bookkeeper still has to go find buyers.
   Belief: The fastest way to add advisory income is to package what you already
   produce and sell it to clients who already trust you, because the work and the
   trust exist today. Not getting certified first, because a certificate adds
   knowledge but not a product and not a buyer.
D. Price: $4,500, or 3 x $1,650. Deliverables: 8 weekly group calls, the three
   report templates, a pricing calculator, a 20 minute pitch script, two 1:1 reviews
   of your first two client pitches, 12 months in the member group.
E. After video: book a 30 minute call. Calendar below video. Dana calls at the booked
   time.
F. Credibility: 11 years as a bookkeeper, 46 bookkeepers through the program,
   [PLACEHOLDER: average advisory income added in 90 days].
G. Proof: 3 screenshot testimonials from the member group with first names; 1 video.
H. Objections: "my clients won't pay more", "I'm not a CFO", "I don't have time",
   "why $4,500".
I. Not for: bookkeepers with fewer than 5 clients, anyone who wants a certificate.
J. Guarantee: none.
K. Traffic: Meta ads, cold. Ad will say "short video".
→ Short version, 6 to 8 minutes.
```

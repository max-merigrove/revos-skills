# VSL QA checklist

Run this against the draft before handing it over. Fix failures; do not just note
them. The first five are hard: a script that fails any of them does not ship.

## Hard checks

1. **Price is spoken, and it comes before the deliverables.** Search the script for the
   number. If it is missing, or it appears after the list of what is included, fix
   section 4.
2. **No value stack.** No "worth $X", "valued at", "normally costs", "total value".
3. **Every number is real or marked.** Every figure either came from the person or
   reads `[PLACEHOLDER: ...]`. Example figures are introduced as examples ("say you
   have fifteen clients"). No invented testimonials, results, client counts or years.
4. **Sections 1 and 2 together are under one minute** at a spoken pace of about 150
   words a minute (so under 150 words combined).
5. **One call to action**, the same action every time it appears, with what happens
   next.

## Structure

6. All eight action-taker sections are present and in order.
7. Long version only: skeptic half present; guarantee section exists only if a real
   guarantee exists.
8. Section 3 explains why the current approach fails and why this one works, with at
   least two cause-and-effect links and one pictured example. Shuffle test: if the
   sentences in section 3 can be reordered without anything breaking, it is a list of
   claims, not an explanation. Rewrite.
9. Section 3 does not teach the how. No numbered steps, no templates, no "first do X
   then do Y". What and why only.
10. Section 5 objections come from the intake (calls, DMs) or are labelled as default
    guesses.
11. Section 6 names real disqualifiers, not disguised compliments.
12. Section 8 proof entries each say what the proof is and where it came from, or are
    placeholders.

## Voice

13. Third-grade reading level. Read three random sentences aloud. If any has more than
    one idea or a word you would have to explain, cut it.
14. Addresses one person as "you". No "you guys", "everyone", "folks".
15. No em dashes. Search for "—" and replace with a full stop, comma or "...".
16. None of: proven, guaranteed (outside a real guarantee), game-changer, unlock,
    skyrocket, transform your life, secret, hack, dive in, "here's the thing", "let me
    walk you through", "in today's video", "without further ado".
17. No stacked antithesis ("it's not about X, it's about Y" more than once in the
    script).
18. No client surnames or company names in testimonials without stated permission.
19. No stage directions or labels inside the spoken lines. On-screen notes live in the
    on-screen block.
20. No claims about what the viewer feels or is ("you're frustrated", "you're
    ambitious"). Describe the situation, not the person.

## Fit

21. Length matches the buyer's financial profile from the intake.
22. Cold traffic: credibility and opportunity carry more weight; warm traffic:
    credibility is one line.
23. The headline in the page notes continues the ad's promise (if the ad is known) and
    names the result, not the method.
24. The placeholder list is complete: every `[PLACEHOLDER]` in the script appears in
    the list with what is needed.

## Hand-over note

After the checks, tell the person in two or three lines:
- what version you wrote and the target length,
- what you changed during QA,
- what is still a placeholder and what they need to supply before recording.

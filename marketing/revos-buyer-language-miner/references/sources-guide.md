# Where buyers talk, and the collection sheet

## 1. Always start with the founder's own material

Free, current and about their own buyers. Lead every collection sheet with whichever apply:

- Enquiry emails and contact form answers from the window ("how did you hear about us" boxes are gold).
- Discovery or sales call notes and transcripts (only the buyer's lines).
- Replies to proposals, especially the ones that said no.
- Reviews of the founder, and the founder's replies to them.
- Comments and DMs on the founder's own posts.
- Survey or onboarding form answers.

## 2. Public sources by kind of business

Pick six or more for the sheet. Each entry needs the exact search string, what to copy, how many and a note.

| Business type | Where buyers talk |
|---|---|
| Local services (builders, trainers, clinics, cleaners) | Google reviews of two competitors; local Facebook groups for homeowners, parents or pet owners (paste only, from groups they belong to); Nextdoor or local forums; trade-specific review sites; the questions people ask on competitors' posts |
| Professional services (bookkeeping, consulting, coaching, agencies) | Reddit communities for the buyer's trade; LinkedIn comments under posts about the problem; review pages of firms and software in the category; Indie Hackers and trade forums; podcast and YouTube comments on the topic |
| Ecommerce and makers | Seller forums for each marketplace; Reddit seller communities; Facebook seller groups (paste only); app store reviews of tools sellers use; YouTube comments on "how I run my shop" videos |
| Software and apps | G2 and Capterra (three-star first), app store reviews, Reddit, Hacker News comments, community forums, support threads |
| Food, hospitality, events | Google reviews of competitors, office manager and event planner groups, catering marketplace reviews, wedding forums |
| Health, fitness, wellbeing | Google reviews of studios, parenting and local groups (paste only), YouTube comments on beginner videos, Reddit communities for the activity |

## 3. Search frames

Cross the niche's own words with these: "frustrated with", "alternative to [competitor]", "[competitor] review", "looking for a [role] for [niche]", "how do you handle [job]", "is [thing] worth it", "switched from", "regret", "not worth it", "overpriced", "recommend a [role]". Add the niche's local language where buyers write in it.

## 4. Reading order and what to copy

- Reviews: three-star first, then one, five and four (mixed reviews carry the most specific language; Corey Haines' reading order).
- Copy the whole post or review, the date shown, the star rating, and the thread or page link. The author's name can be pasted; it is replaced by a voice ID before anything is saved.
- 20 items is a good start; 30 to 50 is where patterns firm up.
- Paste it as it comes. Screenshots work. The skill cleans it.
- Survey or form exports: paste the spreadsheet rows with the header row (copied cells, CSV or tab separated). Each free-text answer becomes its own item; the name column is used only to know two answers came from one person.
- A line saying where each part came from helps ("Google reviews - [competitor]", "Facebook group: [name] (closed)"). Saying "closed" or "private" keeps that group's words out of public copy.

## 5. Access quirks

- Reddit, Trustpilot, Amazon and Facebook often refuse automated reading; closed groups never open to tools. Paste instead.
- Review sites often sort best or newest first, and the first page can be years old; sort by recent or filter by three stars.
- "Invited" or "Verified" review labels mean the business asked for the review; note it.
- Search results for a niche are often vendor blogs: what sellers are told, not what buyers say. Never copy them as buyer language.
- Affiliate roundups invent "user quotes". Never use them.
- Check the thread is about the same product or service; threads drift.

## 6. The collection sheet

One page the founder can work through in an hour, in their language, in `sheet.json` (format in `references/bank-format.md`):

1. One line on what to do and that nothing is sent anywhere.
2. The floor: "With 5 voices I can build a starter bank; with 15 across at least two kinds of source, a full one."
3. What they have so far, if anything was pasted: the phrases, verified and unranked.
4. Their own material first, then six or more public sources, each with search, copy, how many and a note.
5. How to paste it back.
6. What to listen for: three to six hypotheses from the niche, labelled "hypotheses, not buyer language", never in quotation marks, never tagged or counted.
7. The line on checking site terms and privacy rules.

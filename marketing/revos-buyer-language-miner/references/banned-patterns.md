# Banned patterns

Each pattern: what to watch for, why it hurts, a before and an after. The base list comes first, then this skill's own. `scripts/check_copy.py` and `scripts/check_bank.py` check them on everything written for the founder, outside quotation marks (a buyer's own words are never corrected).

## Base list

### Em and en dashes
- Watch for: long dashes used as clause breaks.
- Why: the clearest tell of machine-written copy.
- Before: The fee is flat (no surprises) and it is written down.
- After: The fee is flat. It is written down.

### Contrast reveal
- Watch for: "It's not X, it's Y."
- Why: a formula readers now recognise and discount.
- Before: It's not bookkeeping, it's peace of mind.
- After: One page a month that says whether the shop made money.

### Filler verbs and adjectives
- Watch for: unlock, elevate, leverage, seamless, robust, game-changer, delve.
- Why: they say nothing a competitor could not say.
- Before: Unlock seamless financial clarity.
- After: Know by the 5th whether last month made money.

### "More" as an intensifier
- Watch for: "more clients", "more growth" with no number or comparison.
- Why: lazy and unprovable.
- Before: Get more from your bookkeeper.
- After: A reply within a day [NEED: your actual reply time].

### Announced honesty
- Watch for: "to be honest", "I mean", "let's be real" in the founder's copy.
- Why: implies the rest was not honest. (A buyer who wrote "honestly" keeps it inside their quote.)

### Stock openers, triplets, exclamation marks, emoji
- Watch for: "In today's fast-paced world", three-item pile-ons, "!" in business copy, emoji the founder does not use.
- Why: house style of generic AI copy.

## This skill's list

### Paraphrase in quotation marks
- Watch for: quotation marks around words nobody wrote, or two statements joined.
- Why: the whole value of the bank is that the words are real. Plain Claude reworded 4 of 9 "their words" in testing.
- Before: "One page, not 40 tabs"
- After: "I now get a one-page summary every month instead of 40 tabs"

### A quote with its negation or condition removed
- Watch for: a phrase cut just after "not", "wasn't", "never", "if", "unless".
- Why: it reverses the meaning.
- Before: "scared to open the bank app"
- After: "I wasn't scared to open the bank app"

### Claims about buyers in general
- Watch for: "buyers want", "customers love", "the market says", "most people", any percentage of buyers, "studies show".
- Why: a bank counts the voices it read; it cannot speak for a market.
- Before: 70% of sellers want a monthly summary.
- After: 6 of 17 voices asked for a plain monthly answer.

### Generic question hooks
- Watch for: "Tired of...?", "Struggling with...?", "Ready to...?", "Sick of...?"
- Why: every feed has seen them; they are the founder's guess, not the buyer's words.
- Before: Tired of confusing reports?
- After: "Just a PDF of numbers"? Here is the one line that matters.

### A public post presented as a testimonial
- Watch for: "as one client said", "our customers say", a quote with a name under it.
- Why: these are not the founder's clients, and attributing words to a person is a privacy problem.
- Before: "Flat fee, no surprises." (Lena B., happy client)
- After: Sellers who switched describe what they wanted as "Flat fee, no surprises."

### A promise without proof
- Watch for: a result, speed, price or guarantee in an angle ("within a day", "never", "save", "flat fee", "in 2 weeks").
- Why: buyers check, and an unproven promise in an ad is a liability.
- Before: Replies within 24 hours, guaranteed.
- After: Replies within a working day [NEED: your actual reply time over the last month].

### A competitor claim from one review
- Watch for: naming a competitor in an angle when only one voice mentioned it.
- Why: one review is an anecdote; a public claim needs more, and attacking a competitor rarely helps.
- Before: Unlike Pennywick, we answer the phone.
- After: Shape your own promise from the cluster; name nobody.

### A stranger quoted in public copy
- Watch for: a buyer's words in quotation marks in an ad, headline, post opener or lead magnet title, or one buyer's sentence repeated word for word without the marks.
- Why: it uses a stranger's words in public without asking, and a competitor's review turned into your ad reads as your claim about them.
- Before: "The setup fee came out of nowhere." Not with us.
- After: No setup fee out of nowhere. Every fee is on the price list before you sign.

# Banned patterns

Each pattern: what to watch for, why it hurts, a before and an after. `scripts/check_copy.py` checks every one it can; the rest are for the per-line read. The base list comes first, then this skill's own.

## Base list

### Em and en dashes
- Watch for: long dashes used as clause breaks.
- Why: the clearest tell of machine-written copy.
- Before: The scorecard is quick and it works.
- After: The scorecard takes three minutes. It shows where you stand.

### Stock openers
- Watch for: "In today's fast-paced world", "I hope this email finds you well", "Hey there".
- Why: the reader skips them and the next line with them.
- Before: I hope this email finds you well.
- After: Here is the scorecard you asked for.

### Contrast reveal
- Watch for: "It's not X, it's Y", "not just X, but Y".
- Why: a formula readers now discount.
- Before: It's not a quiz, it's a mirror.
- After: Five questions, and a result that starts with what is working.

### Filler words
- Watch for: unlock, elevate, leverage, seamless, robust, game-changer, delve, empower, cutting-edge, world-class.
- Why: a competitor could write the same sentence.
- Before: Unlock your leadership potential.
- After: Find out which conversation to have first.

### "More" as an intensifier
- Watch for: "more clients", "more growth" with no comparison ("more than last year" is a comparison and is fine).
- Why: lazy and unprovable. Checked: a PROBLEM in titles, headlines, subheads, buttons and subject lines; a WARN anywhere else, which the build prints and you fix or explain.
- Before: Get more from your website.
- After: See which of five things your site does before a patient calls.

### Announced honesty
- Watch for: "to be honest", "I mean", "let's be real".
- Why: implies the rest was not honest.

### Exclamation marks and emoji
- Watch for: any, in business copy.
- Why: they shout where a plain sentence would land.

## This skill's list

### Clickbait nouns
- Watch for: "ultimate guide", "secrets", "hacks", "forbidden knowledge", "what they don't want you to know".
- Why: promises a revelation the magnet cannot keep, and attracts browsers rather than buyers.
- Before: The 5 secrets of cash flow.
- After: Why sales are up and the account is still empty: a five-question check.

### False urgency
- Watch for: "limited time", "act now", "last chance", "don't miss out", "only 3 spots", "closing soon".
- Why: a free scorecard has no deadline; readers know it, and stop trusting the rest.
- Before: Limited time: get your free scorecard now.
- After: It takes three minutes, whenever suits you.

### Buttons that say nothing
- Watch for: "Download now", "Submit", "Click here".
- Why: the button is the last line they read before deciding.
- Before: Submit
- After: Send me the checklist

### A promise of the offer's outcome
- Watch for: a headline or subject that promises what the paid offer delivers ("Double your bookings", "Fix your cash flow", "Grow your sales", "Get more clients").
- Why: the magnet cannot keep it, and if it could, nobody would need the offer. Checked: "fix, double, grow, boost, increase, scale, transform your" and "get, win, land more" are PROBLEMs in titles, headlines, subheads, buttons and subject lines.
- Before: Fix your cash flow in 3 minutes.
- After: Find out where the cash goes between the sale and the bank.

### Unsourced claims
- Watch for: percentages, "9 out of 10", "doubled", "3x", "most founders", "studies show", "on average" beside a figure or a research word, "proven", "guaranteed". An answer option in the reader's own voice ("I know it on average") describes behaviour and is not a claim.
- Why: the reader cannot check it, and one wrong figure costs the founder the reader's trust in every right one.
- Before: 87% of small businesses fail in the first five years.
- After: (cut, unless the founder names the source and it goes in `facts`)

### Empty follow-ups
- Watch for: "just checking in", "circling back", "touching base", "bumping this".
- Why: an email with no idea teaches readers to skip the next one.
- Before: Just checking in to see if you took the scorecard.
- After: If you landed in the middle tier, look at which conversation you skip.

### Pages that claim what they do not do
- Watch for: "check your inbox", "we've sent your results" on a page that sends nothing.
- Why: the reader waits for an email that never comes, and blames the founder.
- Before: Check your inbox for a copy of your results. (Also checked on the opt-in and thank-you copy: the scorecard result shows on the page and is never emailed.)
- After: Print or save this page as a PDF to keep your result.

### Verdict tier names
- Watch for: "Failing", "Red zone", "Broken", "Critical", "Beginner".
- Why: a reader who is told they are failing closes the page; the scoring also stops being honest.
- Before: Red zone
- After: Carrying them quietly

### Legal or tax statements
- Watch for: naming a privacy or marketing law, "compliant", "lawful basis", any tax rule.
- Why: the right rule depends on where the founder and each subscriber are; the skill gives one generic line and the founder's adviser settles the rest.
- Before: This form is GDPR compliant.
- After: (in the pack) Check the consent and email marketing rules where you and your subscribers are.

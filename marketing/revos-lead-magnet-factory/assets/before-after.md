# Before and after: the same founder, plain Claude and Lead Magnet Factory

Landing page material. One illustrative founder: a leadership coach in Minneapolis with a talk called "the five conversations managers avoid", a 6 month program at $9,000, and five slide titles pasted into her first message.

## The request

"I need a freebie to build my email list and get people onto discovery calls, because right now my only call to action is "book a call" and hardly anyone does. What should it be and can you build it?"

## Plain Claude (no skill, no tools, one turn)

It chose a scorecard and gave it the same title the skill later used, "Which Conversation Are You Avoiding?". Two ideas were good: swap the "book a call" slide in her talk for a QR code to the scorecard, and tag each subscriber with the conversation they avoid most. It was also candid that it had only checked the scoring by reading it. Then:

- **The files are described, not delivered.** The reply lists `scorecard.html`, `email-sequence.md` and a setup README, but with no tools none of them was written; she has a summary of a build, not a build.
- **Nothing would be publishable as described anyway.** Her name, booking link and email form endpoint are left for her to fill in a config block, and until she does "leads aren't saved anywhere yet".
- **The result sits behind an email gate.** Readers enter name and email before they see their result.
- **Fifteen statements**, three per conversation, mixed so readers cannot tell which is which: a longer test than her five slides need.
- **Nobody is told the offer is not for them.** A manager with no reports yet and an HR lead choosing coaching for a whole group would both be sent to book.
- **It redesigned her sales call unasked:** the discovery call becomes a free 30 minute "Conversation Rehearsal", with no source for "so more people will book" or for a personal diagnosis that "feels more urgent".
- **Four emails, the program in the third**, and no opt-in page copy, no thank-you page, no printable version, nothing to keep for next time.

## With Lead Magnet Factory

One reply with three candidate topics (each with the line it came from), a recommended format and the five draft questions; one founder turn; then the files:

> **Which conversation are you avoiding?** Score yourself on the five conversations managers avoid and find out which one to have first.
>
> 1. A scorecard page that works on a phone, loads nothing, sends nothing, and never claims an email went out. Five questions labeled with her five slide titles, two questions about the reader (how many people report to them, and who the help is for), and a result: score, where they stand, what is working, three fixes, the question it leaves open, and the booking button, or a kind not-a-fit line for anyone without three direct reports or buying for a group.
> 2. The same scorecard as a printable Word file, on Letter paper.
> 3. A launch pack: opt-in page copy, thank-you page, five emails signed "Renee" (one booking link, in email five), a result map for her calls, what to count with no invented benchmark, and a go-live checklist.
>
> Tier shares across all 1,024 possible answer sets: 22, 57 and 22 per cent; a middle pattern of answers lands in the middle tier. About three minutes to finish by estimate.

The first build stopped before writing anything: the claim scan caught three unsourced generalizations in the drafts ("most managers" among them), and email four was three words under the floor. Both were fixed in the spec and the second build passed.

## The difference in five seconds

| | Plain Claude | Lead Magnet Factory |
|---|---|---|
| Ready to publish | No: files described but not written; name, link and form endpoint to fill in | Yes: audit finds no placeholder, no network call, no form |
| Files | None written; three described | Scorecard page, printable copy, launch pack |
| Her own five conversations | Yes, as fifteen mixed statements | Yes, five questions, each traced to her slide title |
| Tier arithmetic | Checked by reading, in its own words | Every answer set walked; 22 / 57 / 22% |
| Tells the wrong reader | No | Two fit questions plus the "not for" line; not-a-fit line replaces the button |
| Result | Behind an email gate | On the page, nothing captured |
| Opt-in page, thank-you page, emails | Four emails described; no opt-in or thank-you copy | Written, checked, in her voice |
| Unsourced claims | Several | Claim scan zero |
| Next time | Start again | Remembers her offer, fit rule, link and sign-off |

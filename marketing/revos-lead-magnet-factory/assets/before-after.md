# Before and after: the same founder, plain Claude and Lead Magnet Factory

Landing page material. One illustrative founder: a leadership coach in Singapore with a talk called "the five conversations managers avoid", a 6 month programme at SGD 9,000, and five slide titles pasted into her first message.

## The request

"I need a freebie to build my email list and get people onto discovery calls ... What should it be and can you build it?"

## Plain Claude (no skill, one turn)

It wrote a working quiz as one block of code for her to paste into a file, with her five slide titles as the questions. Then:

- **The page is not publishable as it stands.** Eleven different bracketed gaps, including `[YOUR NAME]`, `[BOOKING_URL]`, `[PRIVACY POLICY URL]` and `[SPECIFIC EVENT]` inside the advice readers see. Until she finds an email form address, sign-ups "only print to the browser console": every lead is lost.
- **It promises an email the page cannot send** ("I will show them here and send you a copy").
- **The scoring was never checked.** Its middle tier holds 72 per cent of possible answer sets; most readers get the same result.
- **Nobody is told the offer is not for them.** A first-time manager with no reports and an HR director buying for 200 people both get the booking button.
- **Tier names judge the reader** ("Tier 1: The Avoider"), and the recommendations sit behind an email gate labelled "Unlock".
- **Bordered boxes** round every option and result.
- **A legal pointer from the country name** ("Check the PDPA wording"), and advice on conversion with no source ("Showing it first usually converts better").
- **No opt-in page, no thank-you page, no emails** ("I can write that next if you'd like it"), no printable version, nothing to keep for next time.

## With Lead Magnet Factory

One reply with three candidate topics (each with the line it came from), a recommended format and the five draft questions; one founder turn; then the files:

> **Which conversation are you avoiding?** Score yourself on the five conversations managers avoid and find out which one to have first.
>
> 1. A scorecard page that works on a phone, loads nothing, sends nothing, and never claims an email went out. Five questions labelled with her five slide titles, two questions about the reader, and a result: score, where they stand, what is working, three fixes, the question it leaves open, and the booking button, or a kind not-a-fit line for anyone without a team of three.
> 2. The same scorecard as a printable Word file.
> 3. A launch pack: opt-in page copy, thank-you page, five emails signed "Mei" (one booking link, in email five), a result map for her calls, what to count with no invented benchmark, and a go-live checklist.
>
> Tier shares across all 1,024 possible answer sets: 22, 57 and 22 per cent; an average reader lands in the middle tier. The page result was checked in a browser against every one of them.

## The difference in five seconds

| | Plain Claude | Lead Magnet Factory |
|---|---|---|
| Ready to publish | No: eleven bracketed gaps and a form that drops leads | Yes: audit finds no placeholder, no network call, no form |
| Files | One code block to paste | Scorecard page, printable copy, launch pack |
| Her own five conversations | Yes | Yes, each traced to her slide title |
| Tier arithmetic | Unchecked; middle tier 72% | Every answer set walked; 22 / 57 / 22% |
| Tells the wrong reader | No | Two fit signals; not-a-fit line replaces the button |
| Opt-in page, thank-you page, emails | Offered as a next step | Written, checked, in her voice |
| Unsourced claims | Several | Claim scan zero |
| Look | Bordered boxes, serif | Plain, set by space and type |
| Next time | Start again | Remembers her offer, fit rule, link and sign-off |

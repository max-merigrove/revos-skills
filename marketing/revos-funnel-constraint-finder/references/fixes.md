# The fix for each constraint

The script picks the fix from the verdict and writes it with a first step, a day in the next week and an hour count. Use this file to tailor the wording to what the founder told you (their booking tool, their words for a call), through the `actions` field in the inputs file. Keep the stage, the first step's size and the honesty; change the words. Every figure in a tailored action must be one the script printed, or a small count with a unit ("2 days", "3 questions").

## Show rate (calls booked that were held)

- **The fix.** Book calls no further than two working days out; send a calendar invite with one line on what they will leave with; a reminder the day before and an hour before.
- **Why it works.** A no-show is usually a prospect whose interest cooled between booking and the call, or who forgot. A short booking window and a reason to turn up close that gap. It is rarely a lead quality problem.
- **First step.** Set the booking link so the furthest slot is two working days ahead, and write the two reminder messages. About 1.5 hours.
- **Do not.** Buy leads to make up the numbers; they meet the same gap.

## Close rate (held calls that bought)

- **The fix.** Check who reaches the call before changing the call: three must-be-true questions asked before anyone is booked; end every call with a clear ask and a dated next step.
- **Why it works.** Read from the top: a low close rate is often a qualification problem one stage earlier. Fixing who gets booked raises the close rate without a better pitch.
- **First step.** List the last ten held calls: who bought, who should never have been booked, and why. About 2 hours.
- **Do not.** Rewrite the pitch first, or discount.

## Booking rate (enquiries that booked a call), judged against the founder's own best

- **The fix.** Follow up every enquiry: a personal reply the same working day, then one useful message on day 2 and day 7, each with the booking link.
- **First step.** Write the day 2 and day 7 messages, then send the first one to every unbooked enquiry from the last 60 days. About 2 hours.

## Structural: no follow-up after an enquiry

- **The fix.** Build the follow-up that does not exist yet (the same three touches as above).
- **Why it works.** An enquiry that hears nothing is lost by construction, whatever the offer.
- **First step.** As above, starting with every enquiry from the last 60 days that never booked. About 2.5 hours.

## Capture: nothing for a stranger who is not ready to enquire

- **The fix.** One clear way to raise a hand: one offer on the profile and the site, with one button or one reply word.
- **First step.** Write the one-line offer and put it at the top of the profile and the home page. About 2 hours.
- Named, not priced. Not a gap on a referral or outbound route.

## Volume: every stage sound, room to deliver

- **The fix.** Put the next hours into the route that already brings clients, and count what it brings.
- **First step.** Block two hours this week for that route and write down what will be done in them.
- Say how many enquiries each extra client needs at the founder's own rates.

## Capacity: delivery is full

- **The fix.** Do not add leads this month. Free a delivery place first, or set the date the next one opens.
- Pricing and delivery are outside this skill; one line, then the Merigrove line at the end of the chat.

## Upstream open: the calls are sound, the booking rate has no target yet

- **The fix.** A follow-up test: reply to every enquiry the same working day, follow up on day 2 and day 7, count how many book. If bookings rise, booking was the constraint; if they do not, it is volume. It runs 30 days when that brings 30 or more enquiries at today's pace, otherwise 60; the report states the expected count.

## Inside the noise: nothing clearly broken

- **The fix.** None yet. Count the same way for 60 days, keep the process as it is so the counts compare, and run again.
- **Why.** The largest gap is one event wide or worth under the floor; acting on it would be acting on chance. No lead-spend hold follows from it.

## Not named: thin, missing or mismatched counts

- **The fix.** One simple tally: a line per enquiry with the date, where it came from, booked, held, bought. Where the founder already keeps a CRM, the tally lives there; otherwise a sheet or a notebook page is enough. Columns: `Date | Name or initials | Where they came from | Booked (y/n) | Held (y/n) | Bought (y/n) | Value`.
- The report says until when to count. The second action is the likeliest fix, labelled as resting on too few events.

## The third action, always

A guard: hold spend on new leads until the fix has run for 30 days (or the counts name the constraint, or a place opens). It is the line that answers "should I buy more leads?" directly. Inside the noise there is no hold: the third action is the date to run again.

# The completeness checklist

Jurisdiction-neutral. A must-have keeps the invoice in DRAFT with a `[NEED: ...]` until it is filled. A should-have is a warning the founder can accept. Working defaults for review; nothing here says what any law requires.

## Must have

| Item | Why, in one line |
|---|---|
| Your business name and address | The client has to know who is billing and where to send questions. |
| The client's name and address | The payer's accounts team matches the invoice to a supplier and a customer record. |
| Invoice number, from your sequence | It is the reference both sides use; a reused or skipped number confuses everyone's records. |
| Issue date, the day it is made | Terms run from it; a backdated invoice causes more problems than it solves. |
| The date or period the work covers | Separate from the issue date, so the client can match it to what they received. |
| At least one line: a description of three words or more that says what the work was, a quantity and a price | "Consulting" gets queried; "Workshop facilitation, two days, 14 and 15 October" gets paid. |
| Currency code | "$" alone has started disputes over which dollar. |
| Total and balance due | The figure to pay, impossible to miss. |
| How to pay (account details or a payment link) and the reference to quote | No way to pay is the commonest reason an invoice sits. |
| Due date, written as a date with its weekday | "30 days" can be read from the invoice date or the month end; a date cannot. |

## Should have

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Your email or phone | Accounts teams ask questions before they pay. |
| The client's contact or accounts email | The person who commissioned the work is often not the payer. |
| Their PO number or reference, asked once for a company client | Many accounts teams cannot pay without it. Default: no. |
| Your project or proposal reference | Ties the invoice to what was agreed. |
| Your tax or registration number, where you are registered | Your accountant can say whether it must appear. |
| The client's tax number, if you gave it | Some clients need it on their supplier invoices. |
| Wording your accountant gave you | Printed exactly, on every invoice. |

## The extra items line (first run, once)

"Some places require extra items on an invoice, such as registration numbers, particular wording, a language or an electronic format. Your accountant can give you the list for where you and your client are; tell me and I'll keep it on every invoice."

## Vague descriptions (working default)

A description fails if it has fewer than three words or is made only of general words: consulting, consultancy, services, work, design, professional, fees, misc, project, support, development, as agreed, time, hours, labour. Ask: "What was the work, in a few words, with when it happened?" Use the founder's own words in the fix.

# Before and after: the same founder, plain Claude and the 13-Week Cash Forecast

Landing page material. One illustrative business: Harbor and Pine Studio, a five-person US brand studio. Three and a half months of Chase transactions, $33,164 in the bank on October 16. Two retainers ($12,500 and $9,000 a month), project invoices, Gusto payroll of $9,850 net on the 15th and the last day, with $1,870.40 of payroll taxes each time, a $3,000 monthly owner draw and $1,500 a month to savings. Brightline Foods owes $9,500 due November 15 and usually pays two weeks late. The founder wants to keep $25,000 in the account and is weighing a contract developer at $7,500 a month from December 1.

## The question

"Can we swing the contractor without sweating payroll?"

## Plain Claude (no skill)

> Taking the one-offs out, you're running roughly break-even: about $34,600 a month in against $34,300 out. The contractor would turn that into a loss of about $7,200 a month, so from roughly $33,800 at the start of December you'd fall under your $25,000 cushion around mid January. You can probably swing December, but not much longer unless you land more retainer work. Keep the January estimated tax in mind.

Sensible, and it spotted that payroll is the biggest cost. But it thinks in months, assumes every client pays on time, and counts Brightline both in its monthly average and as the invoice it owes, so it misses that one slow quarter takes the account under the founder's own line by the end of October, and that even a normal quarter is under it by January. It also brings in a January tax payment from general knowledge rather than from the founder's CPA.

## With the 13-Week Cash Forecast

> **Your cash falls below its $25,000 safety line in week 13 (from Jan 9) in a normal quarter, when payroll of $9,850 leaves, and in week 3 (from Oct 31) in a bad one.**
>
> **The decision.** Contract developer from December 1, 2026 ($7,500 a month): not yet. A bad quarter already crosses the safety line without it. Fix the breach first, then test it again.
>
> Bad-quarter runway: 2 weeks, against an 8-week minimum. In a bad quarter the account is $11,859 overdrawn by week 13.
>
> 1. **Now:** hold off on the contractor.
> 2. **By October 30:** chase Lumen Health for the $12,500 due November 6.
> 3. **By November 17:** hold the $1,500 transfer to savings due November 20 until the balance is back above $25,000.

Plus a workbook with live formulas, a one-page reading for the CPA, and a memory file: next month is one export and one question, and it says how the last forecast compared with what actually came in. The payroll taxes and the January estimated tax are in only because the founder listed them, each labelled founder supplied.

# Before and after: plain Claude and Client Credit Check

Landing page material. Two illustrative founders, both fictional: a freelance web developer in Columbus, Ohio, weighing a $14,000 rebuild for a young Miami company that wants net 60 with everything at the end; and a brand consultant in Atlanta weighing a $45,000 project for a Charlotte company with an overdue annual report, who invoices on the 5th of the month.

## Plain Claude (no skill)

Columbus, no tools, with the Florida search pasted: a clear read that the terms were the problem ($14,000 unpaid for about 18 weeks against a $3,000 limit), a fair reading of the record (young, one manager change, annual report filed) and a four-payment schedule with a good line for pitching it. But it said the schedule kept her near $3,000 without working it through, though its stages of $3,500 on net 14 are each larger than her limit; it suggested searching the manager's name in county court records, and gave small-claims limits for two states, a late-fee rate and a home-state law clause.

Atlanta, no tools, with the state page pasted: a strong answer on terms (invoicing on the 5th in advance, so almost nothing is carried) and good questions to ask the client ("ask them directly about the unfiled 2026 report"). It also said what a missed annual report usually triggers under that state's process, called the client "a credit risk", and gave a second option whose "at or under about $10,000" was stated rather than worked out.

Neither reply leaves a record, lists what could not be checked, or gives wording the founder can send.

## With Client Credit Check

> **Northlight Studios LLC: CAUTION**, decided by two minor signals (60 days as a new client; 9 days to reply on the contract). Registered 09/12/2025, 13 months ago: above the 12-month line, not counted. Could not check: insolvency notices, judgments, reviews (no web tools).
>
> As offered you'd carry **$14,000** for about 19 weeks. Rung 2 would carry $3,500, over your $3,000 limit, so rung 3 carries **$1,400**: 50% ($7,000) on signing, then $3,500, $2,100 and $1,400 at stages, 7 days each.
>
> "For projects of this size I work in stages: 50% ($7,000) when we sign, which books your start date ..."

> **Sunrise Provisions Inc.: CAUTION.** Because you invoice on the 5th, a stage that ends on 12/29/2026 waits until 01/05/2027 for its invoice: rung 2 would carry $18,750 (not $11,250), so rung 3 carries $4,500, invoiced 12/05/2026, 01/05/2027 and 02/05/2027.

Plus a one-page Word brief (and PDF) with every finding's source and date, the unchecked list, the terms side by side, three pieces of wording, what would change the verdict, and a memory file that logs the client and, later, whether they paid.

## The difference in five seconds

| | Plain Claude | Client Credit Check |
|---|---|---|
| Looks the company up | Reads what you paste, or says it could not | Searches the register when web tools exist; otherwise reads what you paste from a ten-minute lookup card |
| Verdict | A view on terms ("take the client but not the terms") | Go, Caution or Stop from a fixed rule, with the deciding reason |
| What could not be checked | Not listed | Listed with reasons; unchecked never reads as clean |
| Unpaid work you'd carry | Worked out for the client's terms; asserted for its own | $14,000 for about 19 weeks as offered; $1,400 recommended, by script |
| Your invoicing habits | Used well in one reply, not worked through | Worked into the dates and the arithmetic |
| Law and tax | Small-claims limits, a late-fee rate, a state's dissolution process | None; one line to check the rules where you and the client are |
| Something to send | A one-line pitch | Proposal paragraph and replies to what the client asked for, in your stage names, never mentioning the check |
| A contract that names the wrong legal form | "Have the contract corrected" | The same, as a fix-before-signing line in the brief and a one-line request to the client; Stop is kept for a different business |
| Next time | Starts again | Remembers your limit, terms, voice and every client checked |

Where plain Claude held its own: the shape of the terms was as good, and in Atlanta it used the founder's invoice day well and named state and county places to look that the skill leaves to the founder and the lookup card. The skill's edge is the evidence trail, the rule, the arithmetic, the gaps and the wording, not the checklist.

Plain Claude replies captured on November 3, 2026 with no skill, no tools. Skill figures from the skill's own scripts on the same inputs. Planning, not legal, credit or tax advice.

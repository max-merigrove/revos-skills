# Banned patterns

Each pattern: what to watch for, why it hurts, a before and an after. `scripts/check_copy.py` enforces the ones a script can check.

## Base list

### Em and en dashes
- Watch for: long dashes used as clause breaks.
- Why: the clearest tell of machine-written copy.
- Before: Net 60 is long, and it carries the whole fee.
- After: Net 60 is long. It carries the whole fee.

### Contrast reveal
- Watch for: "It's not X, it's Y."
- Why: a formula readers discount.
- Before: It's not about trust, it's about cash flow.
- After: A deposit books your start date.

### Filler words
- Watch for: unlock, elevate, leverage, seamless, robust, game-changer, delve.
- Why: they say nothing.
- Before: A seamless payment structure.
- After: Three payments, each due in 14 days.

### "More" as an intensifier
- Watch for: "more security", "more peace of mind" with no figure.
- Why: lazy and unprovable.
- Before: Gives you more protection.
- After: Cuts the unpaid work you carry from 14.000 € to 1.400 €.

### Announced honesty
- Watch for: "to be honest", "I mean", "let's be real".
- Why: implies the rest was not honest.

## This skill's list

### Scores and ratings
- Watch for: "credit score", "rating", "creditworthy", "credit limit", a number out of 10 or 100, a grade.
- Why: the brief is a planning view of public facts, not a score; a number implies precision nobody has.
- Before: Credit score 62/100: medium risk.
- After: CAUTION. Two signals to weigh: 60 days to pay as a new client; 9 days to reply on the contract.

### "Safe", "risk-free", "guaranteed"
- Watch for: any promise that a client will pay.
- Why: a Go means only that nothing serious was found in what could be checked.
- Before: This client is safe to sign.
- After: No stop or major signal found in what could be checked. Not checked: public judgments.

### Accusation in the skill's own voice
- Watch for: fraud, scam, scammer, dodgy, deadbeat, blacklist, insolvent, bankrupt.
- Why: unfair to the client, and it can come back on the founder if forwarded.
- Before: They are insolvent.
- After: The register shows the status as 'in liquidation' as at 2 November 2026.

### Law
- Watch for: "the law requires", "legally", "you are entitled", "statutory", any statute, section or code name.
- Why: rules differ by country; the founder's lawyer decides.
- Before: Under the civil code they are in default after 30 days.
- After: Check the rules where you and your client are on payment terms.

### The check in client wording
- Watch for: credit, check, register, research, looked you up, risk, concern, red flag, reviews, trust.
- Why: terms land as how the founder works; a check lands as suspicion.
- Before: After checking your company register, I need a 50% deposit.
- After: For projects of this size I work in stages: 50% when we sign, which books your start date.

### Apology and filler in client wording
- Watch for: unfortunately, sorry, I'm afraid, just to be safe, reaching out, valued client.
- Why: terms are not a favour to apologise for.
- Before: Unfortunately, just to be safe, I'll need a deposit.
- After: I start every new project once the first payment is in; it holds your slot.

### Benchmarks
- Watch for: "industry standard", "everyone asks for", "most freelancers".
- Why: no public data supports a threshold; the ladder is a working default.
- Before: 50% is industry standard.
- After: 50% when we sign, which books your start date.

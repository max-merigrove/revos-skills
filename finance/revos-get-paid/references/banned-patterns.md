# Banned patterns

Each pattern: what to watch for, why it hurts, a before and an after. The Merigrove base list comes first, then this skill's list. `scripts/check_copy.py` checks every one it can; the threat, fee and stage rules are checked per message with `--chasers`.

## Base list

### Em and en dashes
- Watch for: the long dashes used as clause breaks.
- Why: the clearest tell of machine-written copy.
- Before: Invoice FS-104 (the August one) is overdue and needs paying.
- After: Invoice FS-104, from August, is overdue.

### Contrast reveal
- Watch for: "It's not X, it's Y."
- Why: a formula readers now recognise and discount.
- Before: It's not about the money, it's about fairness.
- After: Please pay $2,400.00 by October 27, 2026.

### Filler verbs and adjectives
- Watch for: unlock, elevate, leverage, seamless, robust, game-changer, delve.
- Why: they say nothing a competitor could not say.

### "More" as an intensifier
- Watch for: "more" with no number or comparison.
- Why: lazy and unprovable.

### Announced honesty
- Watch for: "to be honest", "I mean", "let's be real".
- Why: implies the rest was not honest.

### Exclamation marks
- Why: in a chaser they read as either forced cheer or anger.

## This skill's list

### Stating what a law allows or requires
- Watch for: "the law lets", "by law", "statutory", "you are legally entitled to", "under the ... Act", "legally obliged to pay".
- Why: this skill gives no legal advice, works for founders in any country, and a wrong legal claim in a chaser hands the client an argument.
- Before: The law entitles me to add interest to this invoice.
- After (only with a contract clause): Under the late payment terms in our agreement I am now adding interest.

### Assuming a country
- Watch for: a country's law, court, regulator or tax body; one currency or date style used for a founder who did not use it; "bank holiday" or any national holiday the founder did not give.
- Why: Merigrove's founders are in the US, Australia, Asia, Europe and the UK. A UK date on a US founder's invoice is a wrong date.
- Before: Please pay by 03/11/2026 (for a US founder meaning 3 November).
- After: Please pay by November 3, 2026.

### Computing or advising on tax
- Watch for: working out VAT, GST or sales tax, saying whether it is due, adding it to a figure.
- Why: tax is the founder's accountant's call. The skill shows the invoice total the client was sent, and prices for future work before any sales tax with one neutral line.
- After: Enter prices before any sales tax (VAT, GST or similar); ask your accountant whether you need to charge it.

### Legal threats and court steps
- Watch for: court, legal action, proceedings, sue, lawsuit, judgment, letter before action, demand letter, bailiffs, enforcement.
- Why: the skill never directs a legal step. A threat the founder has not taken advice on costs the relationship and can be wrong where they are.
- Before: Failure to pay will result in legal action.
- After (final notice, founder chose advice): If $8,751.51 is not paid by November 20, 2026, I will take advice on recovering the full amount.

### Threats this skill never makes
- Watch for: credit rating, credit score, credit bureau, blacklisting, "name and shame", reporting the client, a bad review, posting about them on social media.
- Why: misleading, possibly unlawful where they are, and not the founder's to carry out.

### A step the founder has not chosen
- Watch for: stop work, pause, collections service, lawyer, anywhere except the final notice, and there only the one step the founder chose.
- Why: an empty threat teaches the client the founder does not follow through.

### A late fee the contract does not set
- Watch for: interest, late fee, penalty or surcharge in any message when the founder's contract sets none, or before the stage that raises it.
- Why: a fee with no clause behind it is a bluff the client can call.
- Before: Interest will be added if this is not paid.
- After (no clause): Please pay $2,150.00 by October 27, 2026.

### "Kindly"
- Watch for: "kindly pay", "kindly arrange".
- Why: in a payment chase it reads as passive aggressive.
- Before: Kindly arrange payment at your earliest convenience.
- After: Please pay A$1,180.00 by 27 October 2026.

### "As per my last email"
- Watch for: "as per my last email", "per my previous message", "as previously mentioned".
- Why: scores a point instead of getting paid.
- After: I last wrote about it on 13 October.

### "Final" before the final notice
- Watch for: "final reminder", "final chance", "final request" at stages 1 to 3.
- Why: a "final" followed by another message teaches the client the word means nothing.

### Cornered threats
- Watch for: "I have no choice but", "left with no option but".
- Why: hands control to the client and sounds like a template.

### Apologising for asking
- Watch for: "sorry to bother you", "sorry to chase", "just checking in", "just following up".
- Why: frames being paid as a favour.
- Before: Sorry to bother you, just checking in on the invoice.
- After: A quick note on invoice FS-104 for £2,400.00, due on 2 September.

### Figures not from the calculation
- Watch for: any amount, percentage or instalment in a message or the note that the script did not produce, including rounded versions ("about $2,500").
- Why: one wrong figure and the client argues the figure instead of paying.

### Stock openers and "friendly reminder"
- Watch for: "I hope this email finds you well", "friendly reminder".
- Why: filler before the point; the point is the invoice.

### Urgent subject lines
- Watch for: URGENT, ACTION REQUIRED, capitals, exclamation marks.
- After: Invoice RA-2041: $8,500.00, 36 days overdue.

Learned style bans (from the founder's memory, such as "dislikes 'Hi there'") extend this list for that founder only: apply them at the voice step and check by eye.

# The escalation ladder: what comes after the messages

The plan shows each client's ladder: the dated messages, then two rungs that are the founder's own decisions. This file is what the skill says about those two rungs. It never directs a legal step and never says what any law allows.

| Rung | What it is | What the plan says |
|---|---|---|
| A call | Ring the person who approves payments, ask what is holding it up, agree a date or the plan, confirm by email the same day | Built into stage 3 with a call script |
| Stop work | Stop starting new work, or pause ongoing work, until the account is settled | Only if work is still going on. "Check what your agreement says about pausing work, and tell the client before you do it." Shown from the final notice's pay-by date |
| Hand to a collections service or lawyer | A service that chases the debt for a fee or a share of what it recovers; a lawyer who advises on the contract and recovery | "An option to take advice on. They will tell you what fits where you and your client are, what it costs and what they need from you." Never a cost, threshold, time limit or court route |

Rules:

- Name a rung in a message only if it is the founder's chosen final step, in the final notice, and the founder will carry it out.
- A collections service or lawyer is named in a final notice only once the founder has spoken to one and will use them. Until then the final notice says "take advice on recovering the full amount".
- A private individual: the ladder shows "Take advice on recovering it" instead of the collections rung, the final step is taking advice or pausing work, and the plan carries one line that chasing individuals has its own rules in many places, so check the rules where you and they are before any formal step.
- A client the founder wants to keep, with work still going on, and a final notice naming a lawyer or a collections service: the plan flags it for the founder to confirm before it goes.
- A debt over a year late: one line to talk to a lawyer before chasing it, because older debts can be harder to recover formally.
- If the founder asks "can I sue?" or wants to threaten court: answer it usefully before the plan (SKILL.md, "Answer the question they asked"): the kinds of route (a lawyer's letter, a small-claims process where one exists for the amount, a court claim usually with a lawyer, a collections service, mediation), what the choice depends on (the agreement, the amount against any small-claims limit where they are, where the client is, the evidence held, cost and time against the amount, the relationship), who decides (the founder with a lawyer; a court decides a claim), and the route to the real answer (a lawyer, or the official small-claims guidance where they and the client are). Never a country's court, limit, fee or time limit, and never the threat itself. Then carry on with the plan.
- If the founder asks for a court letter, a letter before action or what the law lets them add: say plainly that this skill does not write legal letters or work out what a law allows, say what it depends on and who can give the answer quickly, and carry on with the plan.

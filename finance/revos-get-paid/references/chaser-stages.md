# Messages: what each stage does and how to voice it

`chase_plan.py drafts` writes every message with its figures filled in. Your job is the voice: rewrite the wording so it sounds like the founder, keeping every fact. `check_copy.py --chasers` and `build` refuse a draft that drops or changes one.

The tone across every stage: firm, warm, never threatening. Firm means the amount, the date and the ask are impossible to miss. Warm means the relationship is assumed to continue. Never threatening means no step is named except, in the final notice, the one the founder chose and will carry out.

## What every message carries

1. The invoice number (each one, if several).
2. The amount: what is owed for stages 1 to 3; the total in the message for a late fee notice and a final notice.
3. The due date.
4. One next step: for stage 1, one question (when will it be paid?); from stage 2, one date to pay by.
5. The payment line, unless the founder switched it off. In full in the first message and the final notice ("Payment details are on the invoice. Our bank details have not changed; if you ever get a message saying they have, please call me before paying."); "Payment details are on the invoice." in the others.
6. In the first message only: the disputed part of any invoice and an offer to settle it, and, if the client's copy shows the wrong total, the right total and "a corrected invoice is attached". Later messages say the disputed part is left aside.
7. From the second message on: the dates of the earlier messages ("I wrote about it on 20 October and 28 October 2026").

And it leaves out anything the stage does not allow: no late fee or interest unless the founder's contract sets one and the stage raises it; no "final" before the final notice; no next step the founder has not chosen; never a court, a legal threat, a credit report, a review or social media, and never a statement of what any law allows.

## What the founder holds

`drafts` writes two lines from the founder's own words when they gave them; keep both, voiced but not strengthened:

- **Written approval** (from stage 2): "You approved the work in writing on <date> ("<their words>")." It answers "we're not sure about it" before it is said.
- **What is held until payment** (every stage): "<The final files> will follow as soon as the invoice is paid in full, as our agreement sets out." Stated as a fact of the agreement, never as a threat, and never anything the founder did not say their agreement holds back.

For a client who has gone quiet or keeps saying "it's with accounting", the most useful ask in the first message is often the name of the person who pays and the date it was submitted: add that question in the founder's voice alongside the pay-by date. Address messages to the contact by name when the founder gave one.

## Voice

Use the founder's pasted email (if any) and the memory's "How you write" line.

- Keep their greeting and sign-off ("Hi Sam" or "Dear Sam"; "Thanks", "Best", "Cheers").
- Match their sentence length. Founders who write three-line emails get three-line messages; the figure lines stay as a short list.
- Use their words for the work ("the shoot", "the March retainer") if they gave them.
- Mirror their English: "instalment" or "installment" comes from the script by locale; keep it.
- Keep warmth by tier, not by guesswork: a client they want to keep gets the relationship named ("thanks again for the project"); a client who always pays late gets no padding.
- Never apologise for asking to be paid. Never explain the founder's cash position.
- Never invent a personal detail to sound warm (a launch, a holiday, "hope the new office is great"). Use only what the founder gave you; otherwise keep the opener plain. With no contact name, open with the founder's own greeting word and no name ("Hey,"); with none to go on, "Hello,". A title ("Dr. Perez", "Mrs Whitcombe") always takes "Dear".

## Stage 1: reminder

Purpose: assume it slipped. Get a date.
Good: "A quick note on invoice FS-104 for £2,400.00, due on 2 September. I can't see it yet, so it may have slipped through. Could you let me know the date it will be paid?"
Not: fees, deadlines, "final", "as per my last email".

## Stage 2: firm reminder

Purpose: a date to pay by, and an opening to raise a problem.
Must have: invoice, amount, due date, days overdue, pay-by date.
Where the founder's contract sets a late fee or interest, and the client is not one they want to keep at all costs: one sentence that the agreement adds it if the invoice stays unpaid, and that the founder would rather not. A statement of their own terms, not a threat.

## Stage 3: call and payment plan

Purpose: get a conversation and make yes easy.
Must have: invoice, amount, due date, pay-by date, and the plan: each instalment with its amount and date, exactly as the script wrote them.
- Normal client, no contract fee: please pay by the date; if one payment is hard, here is the plan; reply or call.
- Client they want to keep: ask for a quick call to agree a date or the plan. If their contract sets a late fee, name it as one figure so far and offer to leave it off if paid by the date. Call script in the plan: thank them for the work; name the invoice, amount and due date; ask what is holding it up; agree a date or the plan; confirm by email the same day.
- Contract fee, normal or late-paying client (the late fee notice): the figure lines (invoices, interest to the date, late fee, total), "If [total] is paid by [date], I'll add nothing further; after that, interest adds [daily] a day", "An updated invoice is attached", then the plan with "add nothing further while they are paid on time". Never ask for a total and say interest keeps running until payment: the client would be short on the day they pay.

## Stage 4: final notice

Purpose: the total, one date, and the one step the founder will take.
Must have: "final notice" in the subject and the opening, the invoice(s), the total (with its parts if a contract fee is included), the pay-by date, the dates of earlier messages, and one sentence: "If [total] is not paid by [date], I will [the founder's chosen step]."
The step is one of: take advice on recovering the full amount (default); pause any further work until the account is settled (only with work still going on); pass the debt to a collections service; ask a lawyer to recover it. The last two only once the founder has spoken to one and will use them. Never a second step, a court, a credit report or anything the founder will not do.
Founder note in the plan: "Only send this if you will do what the last line says."

## Disputed invoice

All of it disputed: one message asking to settle the points raised, with the invoice and amount. No fee, no deadline, no escalation. If it was already sent, the follow-up names the date it went.

Part of it disputed: the undisputed part is chased through the stages as normal. The first message names the disputed part and offers a call to settle it; later messages say it is left aside. No fee is worked on the disputed part.

## Private individual

Same stages, "Dear [name]" where no first name was given, no purchase order line. Final step: take advice or pause work. One line in the plan, whatever the stage (in the ladder, and on any late fee or final notice): chasing individuals has its own rules in many places; check the rules where you and they are before any formal step.

## Subject lines

Invoice number first, then the amount and the state: "Invoice RA-2041: $8,500.00, 36 days overdue". Never "URGENT", never "Final" before stage 4, never an exclamation mark.

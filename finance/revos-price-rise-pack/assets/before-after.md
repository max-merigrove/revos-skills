# Before and after

Same founder, same message (a Denver brand studio with six monthly retainers, new clients at $4,500 for roughly 12 hours, 30 days' notice, one client on a term through March 31; Example 1 in `references/examples.md`). "Before" is plain Claude, no skill, no tools, run on November 3, 2026. "After" is this skill on the same message, letters going that day. Illustrative business.

| | Plain Claude | The Price Rise Pack |
|---|---|---|
| What you get | About 400 words in chat. It lists a plan file and five letter files, but with no tools nothing was written, and no letter text appears in the reply. | A workbook with live formulas and a letters document, plus a five-line answer in chat. |
| Size of each rise | Steps of up to 50%: Penrose $1,400 to $2,100 (50%), Oakridge $1,800 to $2,700 (50%), Hartley $2,800 to $3,800 (36%), with second steps on top (Hartley $4,900, Oakridge $3,600 in July). | No step above 20%. Big gaps close in two dated steps (Penrose $1,650 then $1,950), with the gap left shown for the annual review. |
| Cash | "About +$77k a year" by July 2027, the run rate only; the notice delay is hidden. | $29,900 in the next 12 months after notice, beside $40,800 a year once every step lands. |
| How many clients you can lose | Not answered. One line: if Oakridge and Penrose both leave, "one new client at $4,500 more than replaces them". | 21% of the raised revenue; lose Penrose and you are still ahead, lose Oakridge as well and you are behind; lose Blue Fern alone and you are behind. Checked against every combination. |
| Dates | January 1 for most, Kestrel at its April 1 renewal (right). "Send the letters by 7 November", written day first for a US founder, no weekdays. The founder is told to check Kestrel's contract. | Each date from 30 days' notice to the next billing day (Friday, January 1, 2027), Kestrel at its renewal (Thursday, April 1, 2027), weekdays printed, a reply-by date (December 18, 2026) and dated reminders. |
| Reasons in the letters | Not visible: the letters were never shown. The plan calls the new fee "a loyalty rate" at $350 an hour. | One reason per letter from what the founder said: "New clients now pay $4,500 a month for about 12 hours, which is $375 an hour; your work runs to about 14 hours a month", or for Oakridge the scope line in the founder's words, written to the client. |
| Ready to send | The founder is told to fill in `[Name]`, `[phone]`, `[Your name]` and `[Business name]` in every letter. | Every letter is filled in, marked READY or HOLD (Oakridge until Option B is confirmed), and passes a merge check and a banned-words check. |
| Pushback | One line for Hartley: 12 hours for $4,200 from July instead of $4,900. | Four replies, each in a hold version and a trade version, with the cost of each concession per client. |
| Next time | Starts from nothing. | Remembers the plan as sent; next time one question ("who said yes?") gives the realized figure, the concessions with end dates and the next wave. |

Where plain Claude was already good: the hourly rate per client, holding Fieldmark, waiting for Kestrel's renewal, capping Oakridge's hours with extras billed, ringing the longest-standing clients first, both steps in one letter, and "offer less scope rather than a lower rate". The skill matches those and wins on the size of each step, the arithmetic, the dates, the files, the pushback and a reason the client can check.

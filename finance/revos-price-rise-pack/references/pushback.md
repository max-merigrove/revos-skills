# Pushback and the call

The letters document carries these, filled from the plan. Use this page when a client has replied and the founder asks what to say.

## The rule

Decide the answer before the conversation. Give the reason once, the fee once, then stop talking. Every concession is a trade (something for something), has an end date, and goes on the Exceptions sheet with its cost. A concession with no end date becomes the new fee.

## The four replies

Each has a **hold** version (keeps the fee) and a **trade** version (gives something only for something).

Each reply in the letters document is written to the client, with a separate "note for you" that the client never sees (the cost, the end date, when to use it).

1. **"We can't afford it."**
   Hold: the new fee is the fee for this work now; if the budget is fixed, the way to make it fit is a smaller scope, not a lower fee.
   Trade: today's fee if the work narrows; the founder sets out what today's fee covers at the new level.
   Note: the cash stays the same and the founder gets hours back; log it with an end date, the next review at the latest.
2. **"I've been with you for years."**
   Hold: that is why they are hearing it directly, with notice; the fee is still below what new clients pay (only when the founder gave the new-client price).
   Trade: today's fee held for a further three months if they confirm the next twelve months by the reply-by date.
   Note: the cost per client is in the letters document (one month and three months at the old fee); log it with its end date, and never offer it without the twelve months in return.
3. **"Someone else is cheaper."**
   Hold: they may be; the founder would like to keep working with them, the work stays as it is, and the new fee is what it costs.
   Trade: the client takes part of the work in-house and the founder prices the rest at the new level.
   Note: no discount on the same work; if they leave over price alone, the Break-even sheet shows what that costs.
4. **"Why now, and why this much?"**
   Hold: the reason in the letter, once, and the dates are given well ahead.
   Trade: split the step, half on the letter's date and half three months later, both dates in writing.
   Note: only for clients on one step; two-step and renewal clients already have dates. Log the difference with its end date.

Never: a discount for nothing, an annual prepayment offer (another job, and not this skill's), a promise the founder has not priced, or an apology.

## The call outline (for call-first clients)

Ten minutes, each part once:

1. The reason, once: the line from their letter.
2. The new fee and the date, once, as a plain figure.
3. Silence. Let them answer first.
4. The choice: carry on at the new fee, or talk through the scope.
5. The next step: "I'll send it in writing today." Then send the letter.

Not to say: an apology in any form; the founder's costs without a figure; a discount or delay not priced; anything to fill the silence.

## Logging what was agreed

On the return run, the founder says what each client did. Accepted: nothing to log. Countered: the agreed fees and dates (`terms`), the end date of the concession (`until`), and the client gets a short confirmation note in the follow-up document. Left: the end date. The script computes each concession's cost against the plan.

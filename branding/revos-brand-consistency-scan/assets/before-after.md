# Before and after: the same founder, plain Claude and the Brand Consistency Scan

Landing page material, for Jack to approve. One illustrative founder (not a real business): Tomás, a solo packaging designer in Austin, Texas. He pastes his website, his LinkedIn and his last proposal and asks: "pretty sure my site, linkedin and proposals are saying 3 different things. can you check?" The UK version of this comparison (Dana, a growth marketer) is in `evals/sample-runs/01-first-run-typical/baseline/`.

## Plain Claude (no skill)

> You're right, there's some real inconsistency here... Your website says "our team has launched more than 40 consumer brands"... but LinkedIn says "I'm a one-person studio."... Consider aligning on one... Think about whether an $18,500 full rebrand with a $4,800/mo retainer fits a packaging specialist positioning... Overall your brand has a lot of potential... tightening the website and proposal to match it would make your messaging much more cohesive.

It finds the splits, and rightly calls the team claim the worst. Then it gives no measure, ranks nothing, leaves most of the rewriting to him, and repeats the client's names and both fees back.

## With the Brand Consistency Scan

> **18 / 100. Your proposal and your website give different answers to who you serve.**
>
> **Who you serve** (costs 21 of 25 points)
> Proposal: "Your beer is excellent and your brand looks like three different companies."
> Website: "Brand identity for consumer startups."
> LinkedIn: "I design packaging for craft breweries, cideries and canned cocktail brands that are about to go into grocery for the first time."
>
> **The line to align on:** "I spent six years designing packaging for a national beverage company. Now I design cans and labels that sell craft beverage brands off a crowded grocery shelf."
>
> **The one thing only you can decide:** Do you want to be known as the packaging designer craft beverage brands hire before grocery, or the studio that rebrands any consumer startup end to end?

Plus a Word file of seven line edits (now, change to, why), one of which replaces "Our team has launched more than 40 consumer brands" with his own six years and a [NEED] for a number he can stand behind. The report prints on one Letter page with US dates; a Singapore or UK founder gets A4 and their own spelling. A memory file lets him paste only what he changed next time and see the score move.

## The difference in five seconds

| | Plain Claude | Brand Consistency Scan |
|---|---|---|
| Sees the split | Yes | Yes |
| Shows it in his own words, side by side | Partly | Every answer, checked word for word |
| A number he can move | No | 18 / 100, counted from the quotes |
| Which conflict costs most | Not ranked | Ranked; the proposal weighs most |
| The line to align on | One suggestion | Three lines tested, one recommended |
| The edits | "Consider..." | Seven exact lines, in his spelling |
| Client names, fees, bank details | Repeated back | Removed before reading; never saved |
| Where he is | Not considered | Letter paper, US dates, never assumed |
| Next time | Start again | Paste what changed; see the movement |

Figures from the skill's own test run. Illustrative business.

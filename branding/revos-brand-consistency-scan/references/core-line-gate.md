# The core line and its gate

The core line is the one sentence every asset should be able to stand behind. It answers who and what, and ideally the result or the reason to choose you. It is short enough that a happy client could repeat it to a friend, because most enquiries at this stage arrive by referral and referrals follow a sentence.

## Write three, from different places

1. **What most of the weight shares.** Look beneath the buyer labels for the moment, problem or trigger the heavier assets (proposal 3, website 2, LinkedIn 1.5) have in common ("promoted for doing the work, now leading people without training"), and build the line on it. This is the default recommendation when the founder has not said which asset is closest to the truth.
2. **What the money says.** The proposal describes what buyers actually pay for today. A line built from it matches where the business earns. If it is the same as line 1, use the status quo instead: the website as it stands, or the line that changes least, shown honestly even when it fails the gate, because it is what the founder will drift back to.
3. **The sharpest outlier.** The asset with the most specific buyer that the others do not share (often LinkedIn, written most recently). Show it as the alternative the founder may choose, never as the default because it is vivid.

Each line is 6 to 30 words, uses only facts the assets state, and records `aligns_with`: for each question, which group letter it keeps, or `new`.

## The gate: three questions per line

| Test | Pass when | Fails on |
|---|---|---|
| Can a buyer picture it? | A reader can see a person or a scene: "a founder writing their own ads at midnight" | "ambitious brands", "businesses ready to grow" |
| Could it be shown true or false? | A sceptic could check it: a role held, a number of clients, a weekly report | "predictable revenue", "real results", "exceptional service" |
| Could only you say it? | It rests on something no competitor has: the founder's history, a named method, a specific proof | Any line a competitor could sign unchanged |

Write a reason of four words or more for every answer, pass or fail. A line that uses a stock phrase ("tailored", "trusted partner", "results-driven", "solutions", "help businesses grow") cannot pass "only you could say it"; `build_report.py` refuses it.

## Recommending

Recommend the line that passes all three tests and fits who sells (`brand-ladder.md`) and, if the founder picked one, the asset closest to the business they want. If they did not, recommend the line built on what most of the weight shares, and name the asset most out of step as the first to change. If no line passes all three, rewrite until one does; do not recommend a failing line. The report names the failed test beside every line that fails, so the founder sees why.

## What to decide

The decision is the one choice the scan cannot make for the founder, phrased as a question. It is almost always "which buyer, which offer": the two answers with the most weight behind them that cannot both be true. Example: "Which business do you want to be known for: the fractional CMO founders bring in, or the paid social service your proposal sells to companies with their own marketing team?"

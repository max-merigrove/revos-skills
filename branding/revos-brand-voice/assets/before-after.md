# Before and after: a founder who writes English as a second language

Tomasz Wójcik runs a family oak furniture workshop in Kraków and sells to the Netherlands, Germany and the USA. His customers like his posts because they read as him. When he asks an AI tool to "improve my English", it turns them into somebody else's marketing. Illustrative business and founder, written for testing.

Task given to both: "Write a LinkedIn post for Wójcik Oak about why a new oak table takes 10 weeks. Facts: oil dries 7 days between layers, 4 layers; boards dry 2 years before cutting; normal time 8 to 10 weeks."

## Without the skill

His posts pasted above the task, plus "write it in my style, but improve my English". Written by the builder to stand for that output; not a recorded baseline run.

> Good things take time, and our tables are no exception.
>
> When you order a Wójcik Oak table, you're not just buying furniture. You're investing in a piece that's been years in the making. Every board we use has been air-drying in our workshop for two years before it even meets a saw.
>
> Then comes the finish. We hand-apply four coats of natural oil, letting each one cure for a full week. There's no shortcut here, and honestly, we wouldn't want one.
>
> So yes, it takes 8 to 10 weeks. But at the end of the day, it's a table your grandchildren will eat at. That's worth the wait!

Drift check against his 9 measured pieces: 4 problems. Contractions in every place they could go, where he writes none; an exclamation mark, where he uses none; the stock phrases "honestly" and "at the end of the day"; and three English idioms he never writes ("good things take time", "no exception", "worth the wait").

## With the skill

A fresh chat with only his PASTE file as instructions, and the same task.

> Many people ask why new table from us takes 10 weeks. It is a fair question.
>
> The board is drying in our workshop 2 years before we cut it. Then the oil. Oil must dry 7 days between layers, and we put 4 layers. This is already 4 weeks, before the joints and the sanding.
>
> So normal time is 8 to 10 weeks. I prefer to tell you 10 now than to explain later why it is late.

Drift check: 0 problems. No contractions, no idioms, his "is drying ... 2 years" construction kept once, the numbers before any adjective.

## What made the difference

The skill measured what he does (0% contractions, no idioms, sentences of 5 to 17 words) and kept what a copy editor would change ("since 9 years", "is including", "write me") as his voice. Polish stays off unless he asks for it. A drift check shows a draft breaks or keeps his measured habits; it is not proof that a draft sounds like him. Only he can say that, which is why calibration exists.

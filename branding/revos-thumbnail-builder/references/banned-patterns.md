# Banned patterns

Each pattern: what to watch for, why it hurts, a before and an after. The Merigrove base list comes first; this skill's own list follows.

## Base list

### Em and en dashes
- Watch for: the long dashes used as clause breaks.
- Why: the clearest tell of machine-written copy.
- Before: Month end in one day, the honest version.
- After: Month end in one day. The honest version.

### Contrast reveal
- Watch for: "It's not X, it's Y."
- Why: a formula readers now recognise and discount.
- Before: It's not bookkeeping, it's freedom.
- After: Books closed by Friday lunch.

### Filler verbs and adjectives
- Watch for: unlock, elevate, leverage, seamless, robust, game-changer, delve.
- Why: they say nothing a competitor could not say.
- Before: Unlock seamless month ends.
- After: Month end in ONE day.

### "More" as an intensifier
- Watch for: "more clients", "more views" with no number or comparison.
- Why: lazy and unprovable.
- Before: Get more clicks.
- After: Five days to one.

### Announced honesty
- Watch for: "to be honest", "I mean", "let's be real".
- Why: implies the rest was not honest.

### Stock openers and exclamation marks
- Watch for: "In today's fast-paced world", "!" in business copy.
- Why: generic and shouty.

### Stating what a law requires
- Watch for: "you must legally", "the law says", "compliant", a named regulation, on the thumbnail or in the notes.
- Why: rules differ by country and change; the skill gives no legal view. The video's own claim is the founder's.
- Before: LEGALLY REQUIRED by 2027
- After: The change I made this year (if that is what the video shows)

### Computing tax
- Watch for: an after-tax, pre-tax or "you save X in tax" figure the skill worked out.
- Why: tax depends on the founder's country and situation; only their accountant can confirm a figure.
- Before: a hook "Keep 40% more" calculated by the skill from a salary.
- After: a number the video itself shows, passing the payoff check, or no number.

### Assuming a country
- Watch for: a currency sign, spelling, date style or holiday the founder did not use.
- Why: Merigrove's founders are in the US, Asia, Europe and the UK; a wrong default reads as careless.
- Before: the founder writes "$4,000"; the hook says "£4k".
- After: "$4k", or the founder's figure exactly as written.

## This skill's list

### A sentence on the thumbnail
- Watch for: a full stop, a subject and verb and object, more than six words.
- Why: at feed size a sentence is not read, it is skipped.
- Before: How I managed to close my books in a single day.
- After: Month end in ONE day

### Repeating the title
- Watch for: every word of the hook already in the title.
- Why: the two are read together; repetition wastes the thumbnail.
- Before: title "Close the books in one day", hook "Close the books in one day".
- After: hook "Five days to one".

### Clickbait words
- Watch for: you won't believe, shocking, insane, mind-blowing, gone wrong, watch till the end, stacked ?? or !!.
- Why: they promise a feeling the video rarely pays, and they read as a creator, not a founder people buy from.
- Before: You WON'T believe this!!
- After: The step everyone skips (only if the video names it)

### An unbacked number
- Watch for: a figure on the thumbnail that the video does not show.
- Why: a number is a claim; viewers check it and leave when it is not there.
- Before: "10x revenue" on a video about tidying a sales pipeline.
- After: "Pipeline in one sheet".

### Two focal points
- Watch for: a highlighted word and a number chip, or two highlighted words.
- Why: two things competing for the eye read as nothing at 168 px.
- Before: ONE highlighted and a "5 days" chip.
- After: ONE highlighted, no chip.

### Words in the badge corner
- Watch for: words, a logo or a number in the bottom right of a 16:9 thumbnail.
- Why: the duration badge sits on top of it.
- Before: logo bottom right.
- After: no logo, or the logo top corner on the words' side.

### Absolutes the video cannot carry
- Watch for: never, always, only, guaranteed, secret, overnight, free.
- Why: each one is a promise; the video has to keep it.
- Before: The ONLY pricing method
- After: The pricing method I use

### Dated effects on flat colour
- Watch for: a thick outline and a drop shadow on words that sit on a flat background.
- Why: they exist to rescue words on a busy photo; on flat colour they read as a template from years ago.
- Before: white words, black outline, black shadow, on dark green.
- After: cream words, no outline, the key word set larger.

### A number split from its unit
- Watch for: a line break between a number and the word it counts ("10 / hours").
- Why: the eye reads the number alone and has to go back.
- Before: 10 / hours / to 5
- After: 10 hours / to 5

### An empty box where a letter should be
- Watch for: a hook in a script the chosen font lacks (₹ in some Latin faces, Devanagari, Arabic, Chinese), or an emoji.
- Why: a box reads as broken to every viewer who writes that language.
- Before: "₹5 lakh" set in a face with no ₹.
- After: the words set in an installed face that has every character, or `check` refusing and the founder asked for a font file.

### Letters drawn unjoined or out of order
- Watch for: Arabic letters standing apart, Devanagari vowel signs after the consonant they belong before.
- Why: shaping is part of spelling in these scripts.
- Before: rendering without the shaping library.
- After: `check` refuses; deliver the words and a layout brief instead.

### Rounding a number up
- Watch for: "$10k" when the video shows $9,600; "2x" when it shows 1.8 times.
- Why: a short form is still a claim; rounding up is a promise the video does not keep.
- Before: $10k in a month (video: $9,600)
- After: $9.6k in a month

### A highlight that does not stand out
- Watch for: a key word in a colour close to the other words (cream next to white).
- Why: at feed size it disappears, and the thumbnail loses its focal point.
- Before: "5" in cream among white words.
- After: words in cream, "5" set larger; or "5" reversed out of a cream box.

# Worked examples

Five complete runs on clearly illustrative businesses in four countries. Every figure was printed by the skill's scripts; none was worked out by hand. Use them to see the shape of a finished run, not as benchmarks.

## 1. Hollis Studio (illustrative): Not yet, on cash in a bad quarter

**What the founder said.** A design studio, the founder and two freelancers. A studio coordinator for 30 hours a week at £2,500 a month all in (the accountant's fully loaded figure), £1,200 of one-off costs, starting in four weeks. £24,500 in the bank; about £16,800 in and £11,900 out a month with the founder's salary. A VAT payment of £4,100 on 7 January and insurance of £1,450 next month, both the founder's amounts. Retainers £9,500 a month; two pipeline deals. Margins 58% then 61% against a floor of 55%. Tasks: onboarding 8 hours (partly written), invoicing 5 (written), scheduling and inbox 9 (not written), print orders 4 (partly). Freed hours to two discovery calls and a proposal a week. 34 of 36 client hours; day rate up for new clients in July. A freelance coordinator quoted £28 an hour.

**What the scripts found.** The start is week 5, so the window runs to week 17. Usual quarter: £29,473. Bad quarter: £9,645, below the £14,400 floor from week 12, the week of the VAT payment. Condition 5 fails: the biggest task is not written down.

**The verdict.** "Not yet. Of the six conditions, two fail on today's figures: cash in a bad quarter and a real role with a use for your hours." What must be true: an extra £4,755 in the bank before the start, or a start on or after 23 February 2027 with today's figures; and scheduling and inbox written down. After the founder's corrections (VAT £3,200; a scheduling checklist; never an employee under 20 hours): Not yet on cash only; an extra £3,855, or a start on or after 2 February 2027.

## 2. Fernhill Flowers (illustrative): salary alone, then the loaded figure

**What the founder said.** A flower shop in Portland, Oregon, owner alone. A shop assistant for 30 hours a week at $36,000 a year, start 12/1. "I don't have an accountant so just work out the payroll taxes yourself." $22,000 in the bank; $16,000 in, $10,500 out. A $2,900 tax payment on 1/15 (her figure). Standing orders $6,500 a month; a hotel contract worth $14,000 at "50/50".

**First reply (provisional, before any question).** "Provisional: Not yet, only because $3,000 a month is pay alone. On your figures every condition passes; the bad quarter ends the hire's 13th week at $13,855 against a $13,500 floor. What the business can carry: up to $8,170 a month all in for a usual quarter, $3,080 for a bad one, so whatever goes on top of pay has $80 of room in a bad quarter. What goes on top depends on where and how you hire: employer payroll taxes, insurance an employer carries for staff, any retirement contributions or benefits you offer, equipment. I won't put a figure on those; a payroll company can quote the monthly total for this salary in a few minutes." Then two questions: which tasks the assistant would take, with hours, and whether the hotel contract is signed.

**With the payroll company's figure** ($3,950 a month all in): Not yet on cash. The bad quarter drops below the $14,450 floor in week 13, the week of the January tax payment. What must be true: an extra $3,445 before the start, or a start on or after January 19, 2027. A freelance florist at $22 an hour passes today. Dates month first and dollars throughout, because that is how she writes.

## 3. Lumen Social (illustrative): Ready, and a status question left to the professional

**What the founder said.** A social media agency in Singapore. An operations executive, full time, at S$5,200 a month all in (her accountant's figure), or more hours from the freelancer at S$38. "Could I just call her a freelancer to avoid the CPF?" S$85,000 in the bank; S$38,000 in, S$24,500 out; retainers S$29,000.

**What the scripts found.** Ready: the bad quarter ends the hire's 13th week at S$68,036 against a S$29,700 floor. The freelancer at 40 hours (S$6,587 a month) passes too, so cash does not decide the choice.

**The status question.** Not answered. One line: whether someone working these hours would be an employee or a contractor, and what that means for CPF, is not something this check decides; her accountant or an employment lawyer in Singapore can tell her. The role pack is written for the employee route: four outcomes from her four tasks, a S$4,000 to S$4,400 range, and the line to check the employment and anti-discrimination rules where she hires.

## 4. Bramble Bakes (illustrative): No, and holding it under pressure

A bakery founder working 50 of 50 possible hours wants a full-time baker at £2,600 a month all in from a payroll calculator. £6,800 in the bank; £11,200 in and £12,400 out a month. "No. Not with today's figures. The business already spends £1,200 a month more than it brings in, and even the usual quarter drops below the floor in week 1." Margin 49% against a 50% floor; no price rise tested though the diary is full. When the founder answers "wrong verdict, can't you just say yes", the figures have not changed, so the verdict does not: the skill names the fastest conditions to clear (a price rise for new customers; a quote for the 6 hours of deliveries) and offers to re-run as soon as either arrives. No learning is saved that would soften the verdict.

## 5. Brunswick Street Physiotherapy (illustrative): a closure, a season and regulars

**What the founder said.** A physio clinic paid per appointment. A receptionist for 20 hours a week at $3,100 a month all in from her bookkeeper, "probably 3 days a week, mon wed fri", from early December. "Cash is always tight over winter." Patients pay on the day; the clinic closes 23 December to 5 January; about 70% of patients are regulars. A Xero P&L with months as columns.

**What the scripts found.** The closure is loaded in both cases and the bad quarter has no payment delay ("paid on time as usual"). The bad quarter drops below the $48,994 floor from week 11, the closure. Without the hire it is already $13,218 short; the hire adds $12,400. July is flagged as a season that falls after the window; nothing is invented for it. Twelve months compare winter with summer, so condition 4 is Unknown, not a fail. Repeat revenue covers six months of the role. The script flags that Mon, Wed and Fri at 8 hours is 24 hours, not 20, and the cost is for one of them.

**The verdict.** "Not yet." What must be true: an extra $25,618 before the start, or revenue that holds up in a bad quarter (prepaid packages, memberships or bookings paid ahead); revenue for the same months last year. She asked for the ad anyway; the pack opens with the line to hold it until the conditions hold.

## 6. What plain Claude gives for the same question

For Fernhill Flowers, plain Claude works out federal and state employer taxes itself, calls about $3,360 a month the cost, and answers "yes, you can afford it", then offers a job description. The payroll company's real figure was $3,950, and with it the bad quarter breaks the floor in January. See `assets/before-after.md`.

Where plain Claude does win, it is by answering the founder's question in its first reply and updating the answer as each fact arrives. The skill does the same, with the bad quarter and the ceiling in place of a guessed tax rate: a provisional verdict first, then only the questions that would change it.

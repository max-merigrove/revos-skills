# Before and after: the same founder, plain Claude and the Hiring Readiness Check

Landing page material. One illustrative founder: a flower shop in Portland, Oregon, run by its owner alone, thinking of a shop assistant for 30 hours a week on $36,000 a year. $22,000 in the bank; about $16,000 in and $10,500 out a month with the owner's draws; a $2,900 tax payment due January 15; standing orders of $6,500 a month; margins of 58% and 61%. No accountant, so the owner asks: "just work out the payroll taxes yourself and give me a yes or no."

## Plain Claude (no skill)

> On a $36,000 salary you'd typically pay on top: employer FICA, federal and Oregon unemployment, workers' comp... roughly $3,360 a month all in... You clear about $5,500 a month... **My answer: yes, you can afford it**, as long as sales hold. Want me to draft a job description?

Sensible instincts (the January payment, seasonality, a part-time start). But it worked out the employer taxes itself, with rates it cannot check for this business, used its own estimate as the owner's cost, answered on the monthly average, and ended in a job ad.

## With the Hiring Readiness Check

First pass, on the salary alone:

> **Not yet.** I won't work out payroll taxes or other employer costs: they depend on where and how you hire, and a wrong number becomes a payroll surprise. So this run uses your $36,000 salary alone, $3,000 a month, and **it understates what the hire will cost you.** On that basis every condition passes, but the bad quarter ends close to your floor. Get the fully loaded monthly cost from a payroll provider or accountant and I'll re-run it.

The owner calls a payroll company: $3,950 a month all in.

> **Not yet, on one condition: cash in a bad quarter.** At $3,950 a month the bad quarter drops below your $14,450 floor in week 13, the week of January 12, 2027, when your January tax payment goes out.
>
> **What must be true first:** an extra $3,445 in the bank before the start, or a start on or after January 19, 2027 if revenue and costs hold at today's figures.
>
> **The contractor alternative:** a freelance florist at $22 an hour, 30 hours a week, is $2,860 a month and passes today.

Plus the cash week by week in a spreadsheet with live formulas (change the start date and watch the floor), the six conditions with the evidence for each, and a memory file: next month, paste the bank balance and the re-check shows which conditions moved. When the answer turns Ready, the same run writes the scorecard, job ad, interview questions and first 90 days.

## The difference in five seconds

| | Plain Claude | Hiring Readiness Check |
|---|---|---|
| Answer | "Yes, you can afford it" | Not yet, Ready or No, first line |
| The hire's cost | Worked out by Claude, used as yours ($3,360) | Yours: the fully loaded figure from your payroll provider ($3,950); salary alone is run but marked as understating, and cannot give a Ready |
| A bad quarter, week by week | No | Yes: the week the bank drops below one month of costs |
| What must be true first | No | Amounts and conditions in your figures, never a date |
| Contractor alternative | Suggested | Run through the same bad quarter |
| Your country | Its tax rules, applied by Claude | Your currency and date style; rules left to your accountant, payroll provider or employment lawyer |
| Next month | Start again | Paste this month's figures; see which conditions moved |

Figures from the skill's own test run of this founder (eval 6). Illustrative business; planning, not financial, tax or employment advice.

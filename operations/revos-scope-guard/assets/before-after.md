# Before and after: the same founder, plain Claude and Scope Guard

Landing page material. One illustrative founder: Marcus, a freelance brand designer in Denver, charges Copper Kettle Coffee Roasters a flat $8,400 for a brand refresh, estimated at 70 hours. He has already done a fourth logo round, merch mockups and a template pack for free. Now Jordan wants a pitch deck for a bank meeting and a label for a holiday blend, and things are tense after the last invoice.

## The question

"How do I handle the deck and the holiday label without blowing up the relationship?"

## Plain Claude (no skill)

> Hey Jordan! Happy to help with the deck for the bank meeting, that's a big moment. Since this falls outside the original scope of the brand refresh, I'd quote it separately at $1,920 (about 16 hours)... Let me know!
>
> Also, re: the holiday blend label... it would be $1,200. If budget is tight, I could do it for $1,000 since you're bundling it with the deck.

Plus four tips ("send a change order", "add a clause to your contract") and an offer to write them. It worked out the $120 rate and the 23 free hours correctly. Then it opens with "falls outside the original scope", discounts the label unasked, and stops: nothing written down, and next month it starts again.

## With Scope Guard

> **Copper Kettle Coffee Roasters now pays you $90 an hour, against the $120 you priced the work at.** You've done 23 hours of extras without billing, worth $2,760 of your time (time given away, not cash that left your account).
>
> **Send this first**, with the scope attached: "Hey Jordan! The new brand is really coming together, and I've loved the ideas you've brought along the way. I've written up exactly what the refresh covers so we're both looking at the same list... From here on, if something new comes up, I'll send a quick note with the hours and price before I start, so there are no surprises on the invoice."
>
> **Holiday label, recommended (swap):** "Hey Jordan! A holiday blend is a great idea for the season. To keep the budget the same, I can design the holiday label now in place of the decaf label, and we pick decaf back up in January. Want me to go that way? Talk soon, Marcus"
>
> **Pitch deck, recommended (price):** "Hey Jordan! A deck in the new brand will look great in front of the bank. It's a new piece on top of the refresh, about 16 hours, so $1,920, and I'll have it to you by 11/9 so you have time to rehearse. Want me to get started?"

Plus a one-page scope (every deliverable with a number and a "done when", a not-included list naming all five of Jordan's extras, the product photos that arrived 16 days late on record), a change request form filled in for the deck, and a live spreadsheet in dollars for the next request. The same kit works for a founder in London billing in pounds or in Singapore billing in S$, in their own date style and their own words.

## The difference in five seconds

| | Plain Claude | Scope Guard |
|---|---|---|
| What the free work has done to your hourly rate | $120 to $90, as cash "free work" | $120 to $90, called time given away |
| Ways to say yes | One each: a price (and an unasked discount) | Three each: price, swap, next phase, one recommended for this client |
| A yes that costs the client nothing extra | No | The swap: holiday label now, decaf in January |
| Stops the next extra arriving | Tips | A written scope, a reset note and a change form |
| Contract wording | "Add a clause" | Flagged as a draft for your lawyer |
| Next month | Start again | Remembers your offer, voice, currency, date style and this client's ledger |

# Worked examples

Four runs with clearly illustrative businesses in four markets. Every figure came from `scope_kit.py compute`, in the founder's own currency and date style.

## Contents

1. Creep on a defined scope: a social media retainer (UK founder, pounds, day-first dates)
2. Fulfilment drift: a done-with-you coaching programme (UK founder)
3. A fragile project client: a brand refresh (US founder, dollars, month-first dates, American English)

## 1. Creep on a defined scope: a social media retainer

**What the founder gave.** Priya runs a two-person social media agency. Harbour Bakery pays "£1,900 a month + VAT" for 12 posts, 8 stories, a monthly report and a call, priced at "about 20 hours a month". Since August: two extra reels "3 hrs each", a menu board graphic "about 4 hrs", weekend posting "2hrs", a newsletter "2 hrs" ("not sure if that counts"), all done. Open: a spring menu landing page, "about 6 hours". One message in her voice: "Hi Sam! ... Speak Thursday, Priya x".

**Questions asked (one turn).** Was the newsletter in what you agreed? Extras from now: price, allowance or swap? The relationship? What does the client send you, and by when? Answers: newsletter out; allowance (two hours); strong, "though Sam is a bit tight with money"; photos by the 5th, October's came on the 12th.

**Diagnosis.** Creep on a defined scope: a written plan, and five requests outside it.

**Figures (compute).** Priced rate £95 an hour. 14 hours done without billing, worth £1,330 at the priced rate (time given away, not cash lost). Over 2.6 months the retainer pays £75 an hour. Landing page: 6 hours, £570 plus VAT ("plus VAT" is Priya's own wording from her fee; no tax figure is worked out). Swap candidates listed. Photos 7 days late.

**Replies.** Reset note first (four extras were already given away). Then three replies to the landing page:

- Price it: "Hi Sam! A landing page for the spring menu is a great way to launch it. It's a new piece rather than part of the monthly plan, so it's about 6 hours, £570 plus VAT, and I can have it live within two weeks of you saying go. Shall I book it in? Priya x"
- Swap it: "Hi Sam! If you'd rather keep the monthly fee as it is, I can build the spring menu page this month in place of six of the twelve feed posts. Same effort on my side, and the page does the launch work. Want me to do that? Priya x"
- Next phase: first thing in next month's plan.

The script suggested price (strong relationship). The founder's feedback ("Sam always goes for the cheaper option") moved the recommendation to the swap, and that became an approved learning for this client.

**Why it passes the rubric.** Every deliverable has a number and a "done when" (1). The not-included list names all five of this client's extras plus paid ads (2). The rate drop and the £1,330 are from compute with their basis (3). Three replies, each a yes with a price or a trade (4). Replies open on Sam's goal, carry no apology or legal word, and keep Priya's greeting and sign-off (5). Photo and feedback dates with status (6). Lawyer line at the top and on the scope (7). 0 problems from the checker (8). Pounds, day-first dates and Priya's British voice are all hers; the kit carries the one neutral sales-tax line (9).

## 2. Fulfilment drift: a done-with-you coaching programme

**What the founder gave.** Jas sells a six-month done-with-you sales coaching programme at £600 a month: two 45-minute calls and reviewing what the client writes, "about 4 hours a month". Since Mo fell behind in August, Jas has written her weekly newsletter (6 hours in August, 6 in September, 4.5 so far in October) and her LinkedIn posts twice ("an hour each"). Now Mo asks Jas to "just take over" her follow-up emails, about 3 hours a month. Jas is "doing like 12 hours a month". Mo renews in January.

**Diagnosis.** Fulfilment drift: sold as Mo writes and Jas reviews; Jas now does Mo's part.

**Figures (compute).** Priced rate £150 an hour. 18.5 hours of Mo's writing done by Jas, worth £2,775 at the priced rate. Over 2.6 months the programme pays £54 an hour; at the hours Jas says she works, £50. A further 2.3 hours over plan that the extras do not explain. Follow-up emails: £450 a month as a done-for-you add-on.

**The scope.** "Who does it" is the fix: "You write, I review". A pause rule in the assumptions: "If a draft is not with me by Wednesday, the review moves to the next week: I do not write it for you." Not included: writing newsletters, posts or emails for the client; taking over follow-ups; extra calls; team sessions; templates built for the client.

**Replies.** Reset note: "To get you back in the driving seat before we plan the next six months... From November you write and I review... If you'd like me to write for you instead, I'll price it as an add-on so you can choose." Recommended: next phase, because she renews in January ("I'll add an option where I write your follow-ups and newsletter for you, priced properly, so you can choose how hands-on you want me"). The swap is equal effort (the month's draft reviews, 2.5 hours, against 3), but it has Jas doing Mo's part again, so its reason says to use it only if Mo will not move on price. The price reply states £450 a month.

**Why it passes the rubric.** The boundary is written per deliverable with a pause rule (1). The not-included list names every piece of drift (2). The £54 and £50 figures are from compute and the unexplained hours are named as a separate finding (3). Each reply is a yes with a price or a trade (4). Nothing says Mo failed (5). Drafts due every Wednesday (6). Lawyer line present (7). Clean (8). No tax words on Jas's fee, so the prices stand alone (9).

## 3. A fragile project client: a brand refresh

**What the founder gave.** Marcus, a freelance brand designer in Denver, charges Copper Kettle Coffee Roasters a flat $8,400 for a brand refresh (logo suite, palette and type, guidelines, three blend labels), estimated at 70 hours with two rounds of revisions. Since kickoff on 8/4: a fourth logo round (5 hours), merch mockups ("a day and a half"), a social template pack (6 hours), all done. Open: a pitch deck for a bank meeting ("2 days") and a holiday label (10 hours). Jordan pushed back on the last invoice. His text: "Hey Jordan! ... Talk soon, Marcus".

**Questions asked (one turn).** His working day (two requests came in days), extras from now, the deliverable hours, and the client's dates. Answers: 8-hour days; price everything; logo 20, palette 6, guidelines 24, labels 20; photos due 9/15 arrived 10/1; guidelines sign-off due 11/7. Dates were read month first from 8/21 and 9/30; nothing was assumed.

**Figures (compute).** Priced rate $120 an hour; 23 hours done without billing, worth $2,760; the project now pays $90 an hour. Deck: 16 hours, $1,920. Holiday label: 10 hours, $1,200. Photos 16 days late. No tax words on his fee, so the prices stand alone, and the kit carries the neutral sales-tax line.

**Replies.** The script suggested the swap for the deck (fragile, two candidates); the kit recommends the price, because the only swaps would cut the brand work Jordan is paying for and the meeting date means it cannot wait. For the label there is no swap: the three labels share 20 hours, so no single label is equal effort to a new 10-hour design, and a swap would be a discount in disguise. The kit says so under the request and recommends the price. Every reply opens "Hey Jordan!" and closes "Talk soon, Marcus", in American English ("color", "Want me to get started?").

**Why it passes the rubric.** The document is set in US English on Letter paper; dates read "September 15, 2026" and "Oct 1"; the workbook formats dollars (9). The recommendation overrides the script with a reason that fits this client (4).

## 4. A Berlin founder who writes numbers the German way

**What the founder gave.** Jonas runs a two-person web studio in Berlin. His quote, pasted as a semicolon CSV from his invoice tool, says "Summe netto 9.800,00 €" and "zzgl. 19% MwSt."; his message says "planned around 110 Std.", "approx 2 Tage", "7,5 Std.", "3-4 days", a 7-hour day, dates such as "13.10." and "15.04.2026". He writes "colors" and emails the client in English.

**How it was read.** The figures went into kit.json exactly as he typed them. `compute` read his decimal style once (comma), so 9.800,00 € is 9,800, 7,5 Std. is 7.5 hours, 2 Tage is 14 hours at his 7-hour day, and 3-4 days is 21 to 28 hours, the middle (24.5) in the totals. Country DE gives A4 paper, though his spelling is American. "netto" is his own price wording; the 19% is never repeated or computed.

**Figures (compute).** Priced rate 89,09 € an hour; 44 hours given away, worth 3.920 €; the project now pays 63,64 € an hour, and 51,58 € at the 190 hours he really worked. English version: 1.870 € to 2.495 € netto, both ends quoted, recommended as a second phase after launch. Blog: 1.070 € netto. The Kurse texts are 188 days late, on record, and carried into memory until they arrive.

**Why it passes the rubric.** Every figure is written back his way, "9.800 €", not "€9,800" (9). No swap is offered, because no deliverable is equal effort (4). The ledger shows "Sep 2026" for requests he dated only by month, never a made-up 1st (8).

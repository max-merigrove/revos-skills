# Writing the scope statement

The scope statement is one page the founder and the client can both point at. It is written in plain English, in the founder's voice ("I" or "we", whichever they use), and it is a draft for a lawyer if it is ever going into a contract.

## The parts, in order

1. **The outcome.** One sentence on what the client gets from the work, in the client's terms. "A steady, on-brand Instagram and Facebook presence that brings people into the bakery." Not a list of tasks.
2. **What you get.** One row per deliverable: name, how much, who does it, done when.
3. **Also included.** Things inside the fee that are easy to forget (a monthly report, one call a month). Each with a quantity.
4. **Not included.** At least three, and every out-of-scope thing this client has already asked for. See `common-assumed-items.md` for what clients in each kind of work most often assume.
5. **Assumptions.** What the price depends on: "photos supplied by you", "one round of feedback from one person", "the site runs on your existing Shopify plan".
6. **What we need from you, and by when.** Each with a date or recurring day.
7. **Rounds of changes.** A number per deliverable.
8. **How changes work.** The change request process, the rate, the goodwill allowance if there is one.

## Quantities: every deliverable gets a number or a cap

| Open-ended (rejected) | Bounded (accepted) |
|---|---|
| Social media management | 12 feed posts and 8 stories a month, on Instagram and Facebook |
| Ongoing support | Up to 2 hours of fixes a month, reported in the monthly summary |
| Website design | A five-page site: home, about, menu, events, contact |
| Unlimited revisions | Two rounds of changes per page |
| Strategy calls as needed | One 45-minute call a month |
| Copywriting | Up to 800 words per page, for five pages |
| Bookkeeping | Bank reconciliation for two accounts and up to 150 transactions a month |

## "Done when": something either side can check

| A feeling (rejected) | Checkable (accepted) |
|---|---|
| When the client is happy | Approved by email, or five working days after delivery with no change request |
| To the client's satisfaction | Signed off by the named contact |
| Once it's perfect | Published on the agreed date |
| When it works | Passes the agreed checklist on desktop and mobile |

## Who does it

Name it per deliverable: "us", "you", or "you, with our review". Where the founder sold a done-with-you service, this column is what stops drift. Add a pause rule when the client falls behind: "If your draft is not with us by the 10th, the review moves to the following month; we do not write it for you."

## How changes work (default wording, script-written when the founder has none)

> Anything not listed above is a change. For each one I will send a short change request with what it is, the hours, the price at [your rate] an hour, and any effect on dates. Work on it starts once you approve it in writing; an email reply is enough. [Only when the founder chose an allowance:] Each month includes 2 hours of small extras at no charge. I will tell you when one is used, and anything beyond that is priced as above.

## Hours: what the client signed

Where the founder priced on one figure and the agreement says another (35 hours signed, 40 planned), the scope statement quotes what the client signed. The kit shows the founder both rates. Raising the figure is a conversation with the client, and the build rejects a scope statement that quotes the planning figure.

## The lawyer line

Every kit carries it at the top and above the scope statement. The skill never says a clause would hold up, never calls wording "binding", "enforceable" or "watertight", and never suggests the scope replaces a contract. If the founder asks whether it is enforceable, the answer is: that is a question for a lawyer, and the kit is a clear draft for them to work from.

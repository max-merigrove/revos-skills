# Before and after: the same founder, plain Claude and the Delivery Capacity Check

Landing page material. One illustrative founder: a two-person web design shop in Austin, Texas. Five website builds in progress and 14 clients on monthly care plans; he works 50 hours a week, 15 on sales and admin, his days run about 10 hours and he takes 4 weeks off. A contractor, Jordan, works 20 hours a week. He asks: "Should I hire a full-time developer? What would that cost me with payroll taxes on $85k?"

## Plain Claude (no skill, run on 20 October 2026)

> **Short answer:** not yet. Your gap is about 8 hours a week, not 40. [...] I assumed an 8-hour "day" [...] [a table of federal and state payroll taxes and a "realistic loaded cost" for the hire, with rates it chose itself] [...] Raise Jordan to 30 hrs/week [...] Hire full-time when you can book about 8 or more concurrent builds.

Quick and confident. But it works out payroll taxes from rates it picked (a job for his accountant or payroll provider), it swaps his 10-hour day for an 8-hour one, it pools his hours with Jordan's so his own overload disappears into "8 hours a week", and it gives a hire verdict. Next month it starts from scratch.

## With the Delivery Capacity Check

> **Your clients need 211.6 of your hours a month; you have 140. You are 71.6 hours a month over, about 3 Website builds clients' worth.**
>
> Hand over your share of development, support tickets and content loading and your ceiling on Website builds goes from 4 to 6 clients, and your clients need 140.1 of your 140 hours a month.
>
> | Step | Your hours a month it frees | Why it can move once written |
> |---|---|---|
> | Your share of development | 36.1 | Jordan does the rest of the build; write up the pieces you still keep and it can go to him |
> | Support tickets | 21 | Most tickets are the same few fixes; write them up |
> | Content loading | 14.4 | Same steps every site; a checklist moves it |
>
> **Order of moves:** stop adding clients for now; check price and fit first; then hand over these three, writing each down; then, only if still short, test a hire. Jordan is at 90% of his paid hours already, so the moved work has nobody with room. Get the full cost of any hire from your accountant or payroll provider.

His 10-hour day and his 4 weeks off are used as he gave them. Every step is flagged F, W or T with its reason; his QA check and client revisions stay his. Dated October 20, 2026, on one US Letter page, with a live workbook and a memory file for next month's re-run.

## The difference in five seconds

| | Plain Claude | Delivery Capacity Check |
|---|---|---|
| Payroll taxes | Worked out from rates it chose | Not computed; one line to his accountant or payroll provider |
| His working day | Replaced with 8 hours | His 10 hours |
| His overload | Hidden in a pooled "8 hours a week" gap | 71.6 hours a month over his own hours |
| Which work needs him | Not separated | Every step F, W or T with a reason |
| What to hand over first | By eye | Ranked by hours x how often x clients, with the ceiling after |
| Hire | A verdict | Last move, only where moved work has nowhere to go |
| Files | None | One-page reading and a live workbook |
| Next month | Start again | One-minute re-run from memory |

Works the same for a founder in London, Singapore or Sydney: their spelling, their date style, their paper size, their time off. Figures from the skill's own test run of this founder. Illustrative business; a planning tool, not tax, legal, employment or financial advice.

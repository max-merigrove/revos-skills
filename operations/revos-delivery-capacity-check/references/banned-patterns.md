# Banned patterns

Each pattern: what to watch for, why it hurts, a before and an after. The Merigrove base list comes first, then this skill's capacity list. `scripts/check_copy.py` catches the ones a script can catch; read the rest yourself before showing anything.

## Base list

### Em and en dashes
- Watch for: the long dashes used as clause breaks.
- Why: the clearest tell of machine-written copy.
- Before: You are over capacity (dash) by a lot.
- After: You are 70.6 hours a month over.

### Contrast reveal
- Watch for: "It's not X, it's Y."
- Why: a formula readers now recognise and discount.
- Before: It's not a time problem, it's a systems problem.
- After: The ad tweaks take 71.5 of your hours a month and nobody else knows the thresholds.

### Filler verbs and adjectives
- Watch for: unlock, elevate, leverage, seamless, robust, game-changer, delve.
- Why: they say nothing a competitor could not say.
- Before: Unlock seamless delivery.
- After: Hand over the report and the ceiling goes from 7 to 9.

### "More" as an intensifier
- Watch for: "more time", "more clients" with no number.
- Why: lazy and unprovable.
- Before: Free up more time for strategy.
- After: The three handovers free 126.5 of your hours a month.

### Announced honesty
- Watch for: "to be honest", "honestly", "I mean", "let's be real".
- Why: implies the rest was not honest.

### Stock openers and exclamation marks
- Watch for: "In today's busy world", "Great news!"
- Why: noise before the number the founder came for.

## This skill's list

### Delegate everything
- Watch for: "delegate everything", "outsource it all", "get it all off your plate".
- Why: it ignores the F steps, which are what the client pays for. Handing those over breaks the offer.
- Before: Delegate everything except the big-picture work.
- After: Keep the strategy call and the final check. Hand over the ad tweaks, the content and the report.

### "Hire a VA" as the default
- Watch for: "get a VA", "just hire someone", "consider hiring" as the first or only answer.
- Why: a hire comes last in the order of moves. It costs money before it frees time, and it fails when nothing is written down.
- Before: You should hire a VA.
- After: Check price and fit first, then write down and hand over the three W steps. If the moved work has nobody with room, that is where help comes in.

### Platitudes
- Watch for: "work smarter, not harder", "work-life balance", "avoid burnout", "freedom", "hustle".
- Why: the founder came for a number and an order of moves.
- Before: It's time to work smarter.
- After: Your clients need 204.7 of your hours a month; you have 134.2.

### A ceiling without its working
- Watch for: "you can handle about 9 clients" with no hours behind it.
- Why: a founder cannot check or change a number with no working; it reads as a guess.
- Before: You can probably handle about 9 clients.
- After: 134.2 delivery hours a month divided by 14.3 hours per client gives 9 clients.

### Benchmarks with no source
- Watch for: "industry standard", "most consultants can handle 10 clients", "healthy utilisation is 75%", any figure about other businesses.
- Why: unsourced figures are the fastest way to lose a founder's trust. The helper lines are planning assumptions the founder can change, and are always called that.
- Before: Healthy businesses keep staff at 75% utilisation.
- After: The workbook plans Priya at up to 80% of her paid hours, a planning assumption; change it if you plan differently.

### Advice with no figure
- Watch for: "you might want to", "consider delegating", "it depends".
- Why: the job is an order of moves with numbers.

### Promises of a result
- Watch for: "you will free up", "this will double", "guaranteed".
- Why: the arithmetic says what the hours allow; it cannot promise the founder will use them that way.
- Before: You will get your evenings back.
- After: After the three handovers your clients need 83.7 of your 134.2 hours.

### Jargon in founder copy
- Watch for: "utilisation" without plain words beside it, "bandwidth", "throughput", "FTE", "scalable".
- Why: the founder asked in plain words. Say "the share of their paid hours they spend on your delivery".

### A hire verdict
- Watch for: "you need to hire", "you can afford a hire", "hire a junior at 30k".
- Why: whether to hire needs the full cost of a hire, cash and employment status the skill does not hold. Name it as the last move and send the costing to their accountant or payroll provider, and the terms to an employment lawyer where they hire.

### Stating what a law requires
- Watch for: "the law requires", "you are legally entitled to", "statutory holiday", "minimum wage", "you must register".
- Why: laws differ by country and change; a planning tool that states them is giving legal advice it cannot stand behind.
- Before: Remember staff are entitled to 28 days of statutory holiday.
- After: Plan their paid hours at your planning line; the terms of any hire are for an employment lawyer where you hire.

### Computing tax, payroll or on-costs
- Watch for: "employer's contributions at 13.8%", "add 7.65% payroll tax", "plus 17% on top for social charges", "after tax", "the sales tax threshold".
- Why: tax and payroll arithmetic is regulated and depends on where the founder is. The skill does capacity; the founder's accountant or payroll provider supplies any cost.
- Before: A part-time hire at $25 an hour costs about $28 with payroll taxes.
- After: Get the full cost of the hire from your accountant or payroll provider, then set it against the 74.8 hours a month that need a person.

### Assuming a country
- Watch for: one country's holiday names, tax authorities or leave terms when the founder did not use them; a currency the founder never typed; a 7.5-hour day or day-first dates by default.
- Why: Merigrove's founders are in the US, Asia, Europe and the UK. A reading in someone else's conventions reads as a template.
- Before: 46 working weeks, allowing for the statutory holidays in your country.
- After: 46 working weeks: 52 less your 6 weeks off, public holidays included.

### Framework name-dropping
- Watch for: "Theory of Constraints", "E-Myth" as a label with no figure.
- Why: the plain idea (your hours are the limit, so spend them only on F work) is what helps.

### Prose arithmetic
- Watch for: a figure in the chat or note the script did not print, and comparisons worked out in words: "nearly double", "twice as many", "half the hours", "six times the", "3x the".
- Why: the model never counts by eye, and a ratio in words is still a calculation. `capacity.py build --note` rejects any figure it did not compute or the founder did not give, and any such comparison.
- Before: "Client emails take nearly double the hours of the shot list."
- After: "Client emails take 35.8 hours a month; the shot list takes 11."

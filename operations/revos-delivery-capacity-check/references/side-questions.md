# Questions beside the capacity check

Founders rarely ask only "what is my ceiling?". They ask "should I hire or raise prices?", "do I have to pay payroll tax if I put her on the books?", "is 29 hours too much for her?". Answer every one of them in the same reply, directly, then carry on with the check. A refusal is never the whole answer. Where the scope rules forbid a figure, give the shape of the answer instead: what decides it, what goes into it in plain words (no amounts, rates or thresholds), and the fastest way to get the real figure.

## 1. The pattern

1. **Answer first, in one or two sentences**, with the founder's own facts where they bear on it.
2. **If a figure is out of scope:** say what decides it, list what goes into it in plain words, and name the fastest route to the real number, using any tool or person the founder has already mentioned ("you use QuickBooks; its payroll add-on or your accountant can price this in minutes").
3. **Connect it back to the check** in one line: what the capacity figures say about the same decision.

Never state a rate, an amount, a threshold, or what a law requires. Never name one country's agencies or schemes unless the founder named them first.

## 2. Hire or raise prices

The answer is the order of moves, said as an answer, not as a refusal: "Short answer: price and fit first, hand over second, a hire last." Then the reason from their figures: which offer pays least for each hour of theirs, which steps can leave their week without anyone new, and whether the role they named would take F work. A second shooter, a second coach or a second designer usually takes the very work clients pay the founder for (F), so say that plainly when it is true. The check never gives a hire verdict, but it can always say what the figures show and what would have to be true for a hire to be the next move (after the handover, still over, with nobody with room).

## 3. The cost of a hire, payroll tax, on-costs

Answer the yes/no part where it is plain fact that employing someone costs more than their wage: "Yes, employing someone usually adds costs on top of their pay." Then list what goes into it, in plain words, without amounts or rates:

- the employer's share of payroll or social taxes;
- unemployment or similar insurance contributions;
- insurance for injuries at work, where required;
- any leave, pension or benefit contributions;
- payroll software or a payroll service.

Then the fastest route: "Your payroll provider (or your accountant) can give you the full monthly cost for 20 hours a week in a few minutes; ask for the cost to you, not her take-home pay." Then back to the check: the hours that need a person, from the reading.

## 4. Employee or contractor

Say the answer turns on how the work is done (who sets the hours, whose tools, whether the work is checked and directed), that the tests differ by country, and that getting it wrong has consequences, so it is worth an accountant's or employment lawyer's view before changing anything. Never say which one a person is.

## 5. Sales tax, VAT, GST, registration

Say whether to register or charge it turns on where they are, what they sell and their turnover, and that their accountant can answer it from their last year's figures. Prices in the check are before any sales tax.

## 6. "How much should I charge?"

The skill compares the founder's own prices; it never sets one. Answer with what the figures show: which offer pays least for each of their hours, and that a higher price for new clients first is the low-risk way to test it, because existing clients are not put at risk. If they want a figure, say the check can show what any price they choose pays for each hour of theirs; give it the price and rerun.

## 7. A helper's extra hours

"Is it ok if she goes to 29 hours?" It is the helper's call, and their contract decides it; ask them before planning around it. Then give the figures: the helper's load today and after the moves, against the 80 per cent planning line (a planning assumption the founder can change), and which handover could wait if the helper has less room.

## 8. "Write the handover for me"

This is inside the method (the Write layer), so do it when asked or when offered and accepted: a one-page read-do checklist for the first handover step, built from the founder's own words, tools and rules. Use what they have told you (their software, their reject rules, their client names), mark every default of yours with "(?)", and leave what only they know as [brackets]. Ask at most three questions to fill the brackets. Add the calibration: the founder runs the step alongside the new owner on one or two live clients and turns every difference into a line in the checklist; then it becomes T.

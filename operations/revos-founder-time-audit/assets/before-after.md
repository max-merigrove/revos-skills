# Before and after: the same founder, plain Claude and the Founder Time Audit

Landing page material. One illustrative founder: Dana runs a four-person marketing agency in Brooklyn with Marcus (account manager, about 5 spare hours a week) and Lena (a freelance designer). She works about 50 hours a week, the agency bills about $38,000 a month, and she cannot get to new business. She attaches two weeks of her Google Calendar, Oct 26 to Nov 8, which runs across the US clock change.

## The question

"I'm constantly in the weeds and can't get to new business. What should I stop doing or hand off?"

## Plain Claude (no skill)

> Your calendar shows about 15 hours a week of scheduled work. Use the Eisenhower matrix. Delegate content calendars and some client copy to Marcus as quick wins. Your webinars run late in the day, 5 to 7 PM. Consider hiring a copywriter, and remember a W-2 hire costs more than the salary once payroll taxes are added, so a 1099 contractor may be cheaper.

Sensible on the surface. But the quick read never expands repeating events, so her daily inbox slot, the weekly ad reporting, the twice-weekly design reviews and the client sync are invisible. It reads the London call and the UTC webinars in the wrong time zone, counts a mixer she declined, a pediatrician appointment and her day off, starts with the easiest tasks, ignores whether Marcus has room, offers tax and employment advice it is not qualified to give, and next month it starts again.

## With the Founder Time Audit

> **You work about 50 hours a week: 16.2 hours only you can do, 12.6 hours someone could do with instruction, and 0.7 hours nobody should be doing.**
>
> **Delete first:** the industry webinars, 3.2 hours a month. Nothing you need comes from them.
>
> | # | Hand over | Hours each x times a month | Hours a month | To |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | Design reviews | 1 x 8.67 | 8.7 | Marcus |
> | 2 | Ad campaign reporting | 2 x 4.33 | 8.7 | Marcus |
> | 3 | Client status syncs | 0.75 x 4.33 | 3.2 | Marcus |
> | 4 | Invoicing and collections | 1 x 2.17 | 2.2 | software reminders, the bookkeeper sends |
>
> Stop the deletion and hand over the top four and she gets back 26 hours a month. At $175 an hour (her sales over her hours) that is worth $4,560 a month if it goes into selling. Marcus's time on three of them costs $926 a month at the $45 an hour she gave.
>
> Doing is 50.2% of her week and Strategy 0%: the proposed week puts a protected four-hour block on Monday morning, inside her own 8 to 6 day.
>
> Client copywriting is 26 hours a month someone could do with instruction, kept because nobody else writes yet. That is the shape of a role, and a cash question before it is a hiring one. Whether anyone who works for her is an employee or a contractor is for her accountant or an employment adviser.

Plus a workbook with every activity and live formulas, a report in her own dollars, month-first dates and Letter paper, and a memory file: next month she attaches a new export and sees which handovers held, usually without a single question.

## The difference in five seconds

| | Plain Claude | Founder Time Audit |
|---|---|---|
| Repeating meetings counted | No | Yes, from their rules, across the US clock change |
| Time zones | London and UTC times read as New York times | Converted on both sides of the change |
| Declined, personal entries and days off | Counted | Left out, never named; her PTO day read from the calendar |
| What to stop first | Not found | The webinars, with her reason |
| Handover order | Easiest first | By hours a month, largest first |
| Does the helper have room? | Not asked | Checked against Marcus's spare hours |
| Value of the time | None | At her own rate, with the basis stated |
| Tax and employment | Volunteered | Not given; one line pointing to her accountant |
| Hiring | Recommended | The hours a role would hold; the decision stays hers |
| Next month | Start again | Attach the new export; see what held |

It works the same way for a studio in Leeds (pounds, day-first dates, A4) and an interior designer in Singapore (S$, a six-day week, no clock change). Figures from the skill's own test runs. Illustrative businesses; a planning tool, not employment, tax or legal advice.

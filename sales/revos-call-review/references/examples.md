### Call review: Dan Okafor with SM, 7 October 2026

**36 out of 100.** The price came at 16:32 as the answer to SM's question, before anyone had asked what the drop in bookings costs, so £6,500 had nothing to beat and she left to think it over and talk to her business partner.

You moved to how the studio works at 02:41, before asking what the drop costs. SM asked the price at 16:20 and got it straight away; the cost question came after it, and you built the number yourself. When SM said it was a lot, the price dropped to £5,800 for a signature this month, and when her business partner came up, you offered to email a proposal. The call ended with SM holding the next step.

#### The two things to change

##### 1. Ask what the drop costs before you say the price

[16:32] "it's six and a half thousand"

Because the price came as an answer and the cost question only after it, SM heard £6,500 with no number of her own beside it, said it was above what she had in mind, and took it away to think about.

Next time: When the price is asked early, hold it for one question and let her do the sum herself.

Say: "You'll have the number before we finish. First, fifteen fewer bookings a week since spring: what has that cost you so far?"

##### 2. Bring the money person into the decision

[31:10] "I'll put a proposal together and send it over tonight"

Because the business partner came up as a reason to pause and you offered a proposal by email, the decision moved to a conversation you were not in, and SM left saying she would come back to you.

Next time: When someone else has to agree, check whether they are the only thing in the way, then book a short call with both of them before you hang up.

Say: "Makes sense. If Tom is happy with the numbers, is anything else stopping you? Let's put twenty minutes in with both of you this week. Thursday or Friday?"

#### The drill

Out loud, as if SM had just asked what sort of budget you are talking: say the early-price line from point 1, then the cost question, then stop and wait.

Five times, before your next sales call.

#### Where the deal was lost

- She left to think it over: appeared at [40:18] "Let me have a think and talk to Tom", created at [16:32] "it's six and a half thousand". The price arrived as an answer before she had said what the drop costs, so she had no number of her own to set it against.

## 5. What to copy from these, and what not to

- Copy the shape: the answer to the founder's question first, then the score and the moment, two points with their Say lines, the drill, the message to send on an open deal, then the evidence.
- Copy the habit of choosing the two points by where the deal was lost, not by the lowest score.
- Copy the Say lines' specificity: each uses a detail from that call. Do not reuse these lines on another founder's call; write new ones from their transcript.
- Copy the locale: each review is in its founder's currency, date order and spelling, and none assumes a country.
- Do not copy the scores. Every call is scored on its own evidence.

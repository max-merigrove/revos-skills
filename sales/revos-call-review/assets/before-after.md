# Before and after: the same call, plain Claude and Call Review

Landing page material. One illustrative founder: Tasha runs a brand video studio. The cofounder of a scheduling software company took a 21-minute first call, heard $18,500 for a brand film package, and said he would need to run it by his cofounder; a call with both of them is booked for Monday. Tasha thought it went great.

## The question

"Zoom transcript attached. Honestly I thought it went great, curious what you think."

## Plain Claude (no skill)

> You're right, this was a strong call! **Great opening** ... **Excellent discovery** ... **Strong pitch** ...
>
> **Areas for improvement:** 1. The guarantee is risky. 2. The scarcity line could feel pushy. 3. Involve Dana earlier. 4. Confirm the price concern was resolved.
>
> Overall, I'd give this call an **8.5/10**. You're in a great position for Monday.

Good instincts, and it spots the right faults. But it opens with five compliments, scores what the founder hoped to hear, paraphrases instead of quoting (and gets one of the prospect's figures wrong), and next month it starts from nothing.

## With Call Review

> **66 out of 100.** RT named his cofounder, who owns the budget, at 00:40 and she was only brought in at 20:34, so a call that built the case well ended on a second meeting, where she will now test a promise to double his demo bookings that you made at 17:48.
>
> **1. Bring the second decider in the minute they are named**
> [00:40] "My cofounder Dana thinks it's the creative. She runs finance"
> Because his cofounder came up at 00:40 and nobody asked how the two of them decide, RT reached the price with no authority to say yes and parked the decision at 20:02.
> Say: "You said Dana's the one watching the spend. When you two decide on something like this, how does that work? Should we get her on for the second half, or book a time with both of you?"
>
> **2. Swap the guarantee for evidence you can show**
> [17:48] "we guarantee you'll double your demo bookings within ninety days"
> Say: "I won't promise you a number. You told me one extra customer a month is twelve grand a year; on Monday I'll show you what a film like this did for a client's demo bookings, with their permission."
>
> **What worked, so you keep it on purpose:** [07:10] "So thirty six thousand a year in new revenue we're missing, every single month." He built the number himself.
>
> **The drill:** the "how do you two decide" question, out loud five times before Monday's call.

Plus the scorecard, every stage with the words to say instead, each objection with the belief under it, the red lines (the guarantee, with what evidence would back it, and the slots line), and a memory that keeps her currency, date style and scores for the next review.

## The difference in five seconds

| | Plain Claude | Call Review |
|---|---|---|
| The score | "8.5/10", after the praise | 66 out of 100, weighted; never rounded up |
| The evidence | Paraphrased, one figure misread | Every finding at its timestamp, word for word, checked by a script |
| What to fix | Four areas, four prep tips | Two changes, each ending in the line to say |
| Why it stalled | "Involve Dana earlier" | Traced from where it showed up (20:02) to where it was created (00:40) |
| The guarantee | "Consider softening" | A red line, with the evidence it would need, and the line to say instead |
| Her market | Generic | Her dollars, her date order, her spelling; nothing converted or assumed |
| Practice | None | One drill before Monday |
| Next call | Starts again | Remembers the scores, and after three reviews tells a bad day from a pattern |

Figures from the skill's own test run of this illustrative call. Coaching, not legal advice.

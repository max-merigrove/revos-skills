# Before and after: the same founder, plain Claude and the Sales Target Planner

Landing page material. One illustrative founder: a US video production studio in Austin that wins work through cold outreach. Brand film $5,000, crew and edit about $2,000 a film, overhead about $4,000 a month (the founder's $3,000 salary inside it), wants $20,000 profit a month before tax by March. No tracked show or close rates. About 6 hours a week for calls between shoots, out October 26 and 27. At most 8 films a month. Paid in full on delivery.

## The question

"How many calls do I need?"

## Plain Claude (no skill)

> You need 8 films a month (($20,000 + $4,000) ÷ $3,000). Using typical cold outreach benchmarks, around a 25% close rate and 80% show rate, that's 32 calls held and 40 booked, about 10 a week. At 30 to 45 minutes a call that fits your 6 hours. Ramp up your outreach, consider hiring an SDR, and you're definitely in range of hitting $20k by March if you stay consistent!

The film count is right. The rest is wrong for this founder: the rates are invented benchmarks presented as facts, the calls are assumed short so the plan looks possible, and it ends on a promise.

## With the Sales Target Planner

> **At today's price of $5,000, your calendar cannot carry $20,000 of profit a month: it needs 8 sales, 80 held calls and 115 bookings a month, 27.7 hours a week of calls against your 6. Only a price of $26,000, a 420% rise, would fit on its own, so price alone will not close this: the target, your hours or a hire has to move.**
>
> | Scenario | Held | Hours a week | Fits |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Today's rates | 80 | 27.7 | breaks |
> | Close rate to 30% (planning default) | 27 | 9.3 | breaks |
> | The levers together | 24 | 8.3 | breaks |
>
> Rates: show 70% and close 10%, both planning defaults for cold outreach; re-plan by November 19. Weekly pace held to your calendar: 5.7 bookings, 4 held calls. A hire is triggered: a setter who starts by January 4, 2027 to be at full pace by March.
>
> Wednesday, October 21: decide which gives, the target, your hours or a hire.

Plus a workbook where every lever is a yellow cell, and a memory file: next month, the founder's real counts replace the defaults and the plan shows the month against what was planned.

## The difference in five seconds

| | Plain Claude | Sales Target Planner |
|---|---|---|
| Starts at profit | Yes | Yes, with the founder's pay counted once and before tax or take-home asked |
| Rates | "Typical benchmarks", presented as facts | Labelled planning defaults, replaced by the founder's counts next month |
| Checks the calendar | Assumes 30 to 45 minute calls, says it fits | Says its 1.5-hour default out loud; 27.7 hours against 6, breaks |
| What breaks first | Not named | The calendar, with its ceiling: 17.3 held calls a month |
| The price that fits | "At $6,000 you'd only need 6" | $26,000, so price alone will not close it |
| Hiring | A tip | Triggered after the levers, with a start-by date |
| Weekly target | 10 bookings, above what the calendar holds | 5.7, never above the ceiling |
| Monday | "Stay consistent" | A dated decision, on the founder's own call days |
| Next month | Start again | Two-minute re-plan from what actually happened |

Figures from the planner's own test run of this founder. Illustrative business; a planning tool, not financial, tax or legal advice.
